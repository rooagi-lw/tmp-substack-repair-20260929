onsemi went back to the table and swapped a dilution-heavy stock deal for $123-per-share cash — buying Synaptics for ~$5.7B instead of ~$7B, and Wall Street rewarded both sides.

What happened

  • 📅 Oct 1, 2026, 4:34 PM ET: onsemi and Synaptics issued a joint GlobeNewswire release amending their June 25 merger agreement.
  • 💵 Revised terms: $123/share all-cash, aggregate value ~$5.7B — replacing the original 1.35 ON shares per SYNA share all-stock deal worth ~$7B.
  • ⚡ Immediately accretive to onsemi's non-GAAP EPS upon close. The original deal had been guided as accretive only within 18 months of closing, per the June 25 release.
  • 🏦 Financing: cash on hand plus a fully committed $2.45B senior secured term loan from Morgan Stanley — with no financing closing condition, per Quartz via Yahoo Finance.
  • 📌 Expected close: mid-2027, subject to Synaptics shareholder approval and regulatory approvals. The FTC has cleared the deal; other jurisdictions are still reviewing. Synaptics' board voted unanimously to support.
  • 🔧 New upside: $200M in annual run-rate cost synergies, plus newly identified incremental revenue synergies and insourcing of some Synaptics production realized after the first 18 months post-close, per CEO Hassane El-Khoury in the joint release. onsemi is also no longer obligated to add a Synaptics director to its board, per the WSJ.
  • 📈 Market reaction: SYNA surged 16% after hours to $122.85 and ON rose 6.9% to $85.57 (Quartz/Yahoo Finance). On Oct 2, SYNA closed at $121.54 (+14.5%) and ON at $85.01 (+6.2%).

How we got here

  • June 25, 2026: the original deal was announced — 1.35 shares, ~$7B, both boards unanimously approved, mid-2027 close, with Morgan Stanley as onsemi's lead advisor. The framing, per the joint release: "Physical AI" at the intersection of Power, Sense, Connected Compute and Control, raising onsemi's TAM by $30B to $243B by 2030.
  • June 26: ON fell 12.48% in premarket trading — the market hated the dilution-heavy all-stock structure, per Investing.com via Yahoo Finance.
  • Aug 3: onsemi reported Q2 results and El-Khoury said AI data center "remains our fastest-growing business" with revenue expected to "more than double in 2026."
  • Sept 2: Synaptics received an unsolicited competing proposal from an unidentified third party — dated by an AI-generated summary of Synaptics' 8-K filing on Panabee; the bidder remains unnamed in all public reporting seen. That mystery bidder was the leverage that forced the rewrite.
  • The math that changed: ON traded at $118.74 when the original deal was struck (implying ~$160 per SYNA share) but had slid to ~$80 (implying ~$108), per WSJ figures via Quartz/Yahoo. The revised $123 cash sits below the original headline but above what the decayed stock deal had become.

By the numbers

onsemi's Q2 2026 (reported Aug 3, via company earnings release): total revenue $1,603.5M (+9% YoY). The Power Solutions Group did $829.0M (~52% of revenue, +19% YoY); the Analog and Mixed-Signal Group $545.7M (~34%, -2%); the Intelligent Sensing Group $228.8M (~14%, +7%). Gross margin: GAAP 38.4%, non-GAAP 39.3%. EPS: GAAP $0.56, non-GAAP $0.74. Free cash flow $425.4M, quadrupled YoY. Q3 2026 guidance: revenue $1,650–$1,750M; non-GAAP gross margin 40.0–42.0%; non-GAAP EPS $0.81–$0.93. The companies have not said which onsemi segment Synaptics will report under.

Synaptics' fiscal 2026 (reported Aug 6, via company release): revenue $1.197B, +11% — its second straight year of double-digit growth. Q4 revenue $308.0M (+9% YoY) with Core IoT sales up 43% YoY to $389.7M (33% of total); Enterprise & Automotive $641.1M; Mobile $166.4M. Non-GAAP gross margin: 54.5% in Q4 — far richer than onsemi's 39.3%.

Why it matters

This is onsemi's move from AI infrastructure to "Physical AI." Synaptics' Astra AI-native embedded compute platform, wireless connectivity, and multimodal sensing give onsemi what the joint release calls "connected compute" — extending its reach from the AI data center (where El-Khoury says revenue will more than double this year) into automotive, industrial, and IoT endpoints. The all-cash structure fixes the exact problem that cratered the stock on June 26: no dilution, lower total consideration, immediate accretion — and a 54.5%-margin business that management frames as a "durable funding engine" for its connected-compute push.

Bull vs. bear

Bull: Wells Fargo analyst Joe Quatrochi maintained his Overweight rating and raised his price target on ON from $85 to $100 on Oct 2 (via MarketBeat) — the all-cash structure is immediately EPS-accretive, avoids the dilution the market hated, lowers the total bill from ~$7B to ~$5.7B, and adds revenue-synergy and insourcing upside on top of the $200M run-rate cost savings. The tape agrees: both stocks ripped on the news, and SYNA now trades just ~$1.46 under the deal price.

Bear: $123 is a far cry from the original deal's implied ~$160 per share — Synaptics holders get certainty but arguably leave money on the table, and the mystery bidder's arrival, not onsemi's generosity, may have been the real lever (WSJ figures via Quartz/Yahoo). The unidentified third party could still counterbid; only the FTC has cleared the deal; onsemi is taking on a $2.45B term loan; and both companies live in cyclical semiconductor markets, with the close not expected until mid-2027 — a long runway for cycle and integration risk.

What to watch next

  1. The Synaptics shareholder vote — the proxy statement on Schedule 14A has yet to be filed; meeting date TBD (joint release).
  2. Regulatory approvals in other jurisdictions — FTC cleared, other regulators still reviewing.
  3. The mystery bidder — whether the unidentified third party returns or counters before close.
  4. The mid-2027 close, and whether ON trades toward Wells Fargo's $100 target — plus onsemi's Q3 results (guided revenue $1,650–$1,750M).

📌 Takeaway: An unidentified rival bidder just handed onsemi the leverage to re-cut its Synaptics deal at a lower price, in cash — and Wall Street likes this version much better than the one that sank the stock in June.
