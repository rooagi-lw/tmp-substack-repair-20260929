US Evening Report · Oct 12

Monday, October 12, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Wall Street finished mixed on a quiet Columbus Day session, with the Dow advancing as investors rotated into value and defensive names, while a semiconductor selloff weighed on the Nasdaq and held the S&P 500 just below its record. Volume was light — S&P 500 volume was reported about 55% below its three-month average — because the U.S. cash bond market was closed for the federal holiday, leaving equities without their usual rates signal ahead of a heavy week of bank earnings and inflation data.

The close

  • S&P 500: 7,807.24, down 4.30 points (-0.06%) — just 11.69 points, or 0.15%, below its October 6 record close (sources: Investopedia, The Motley Fool, TS2)
  • Nasdaq Composite: 27,245.90, down 120.27 points (-0.44%)
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 51,850.55, up 195.60 points (+0.38%), a third consecutive advance
  • Russell 2000: 2,794.48, down 0.45%
  • Nasdaq-100: down about 0.71%

Breadth was better than the headline indexes suggested: about 60% of S&P 500 members advanced in a late count, and eight of the 11 GICS sectors finished higher, even as decliners led advancers on both NYSE and Nasdaq in the full listings. Year to date, the Nasdaq is up 17.2%, the S&P 500 up 14.1%, the Russell 2000 up 12.6%, and the Dow up 7.9%.

Monday also marked four years since the start of the current bull market on October 12, 2022.

Macro and policy

There was no major U.S. economic data released Monday, and the U.S. Treasury cash market was closed for Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day, so there was no new 10-year yield print — the last published level, from Friday, was 5.24%, after briefly nearing 5.37% last week, its highest in more than 24 years (Investopedia).

Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said she prefers smaller, more frequent policy adjustments to help keep inflation expectations anchored, while stopping short of backing a rate increase this month (BabyPips recap). Markets showed little reaction.

Geopolitics drove the commodity tape: Saudi Arabia said it had intercepted missiles and drones from Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen after deadly weekend attacks at Riyadh airport, and President Trump said Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an energy ceasefire — a claim Kyiv and Moscow statements left unconfirmed during the session. Oil rose on the supply risk regardless.

Analysts expect S&P 500 third-quarter earnings to grow about 29.6% year over year, according to FactSet figures cited by multiple outlets — a fourth straight quarter of growth above 25%, powered largely by AI infrastructure spending. That is the hurdle bank earnings must clear starting Tuesday.

  • Consumer Staples: +1.16% — defensive bid; Lamb Weston +4.83% after last week's earnings beat and raised guidance
  • Communication Services: +0.94% — Meta Platforms +3.38% did most of the work
  • Real Estate: +0.75%
  • Health Care: +0.69% — a solid, broad gain ahead of Tuesday's Johnson & Johnson and UnitedHealth results
  • Financials: +0.58% — positioned ahead of bank earnings; Citigroup +1.77%, JPMorgan +0.45%, Goldman Sachs -0.14%, Wells Fargo -0.30%
  • Utilities: +0.53%

  • Industrials: -0.06% — essentially flat; data-center power names Bloom Energy -6.47% and Generac -2.92% weighed
  • Consumer Discretionary: -0.51% — Amazon -1.11% and Chipotle -3.75% (after Starbucks, +1.69%, said it is not acquiring Chipotle) dragged
  • Information Technology: -1.22% — the clear laggard; the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell about 2.7%

  • Figs (FIGS): up more than 12%, extending a run of over 30% in five sessions (The Motley Fool)

Unusual volume / activity: overall volume was unusually light for a Monday — NYSE and Nasdaq turnover ran well below average on the holiday — while selling was concentrated in semiconductors and AI infrastructure, the session's highest-conviction trade in either direction.

Technicals and sentiment

  • VIX: 15.21, up 0.37 (+2.49%) — a modest uptick, still a low, complacent reading with the S&P 500 0.15% from a record
  • 10-year Treasury yield: 5.24% — unchanged, last published Friday; cash trading closed Monday for the holiday
  • U.S. Dollar Index: 102.60, up 0.37% — its highest since April 2025 by one Reuters measure; dollar/yen touched a three-week high near 158.70
  • WTI crude: settled $93.77 a barrel, up $1.85 (+2.0%); Brent rose about 1.8% to about $106.65
  • Gold: $4,167.56 an ounce at the U.S. close, down 0.64%, as the firmer dollar weighed; silver fell about 1.0% to about $60.18
  • Bitcoin: about $82,838, down 0.72%

The technical picture is a market holding near highs on narrowing leadership: the cap-weighted S&P 500 stalled, but equal-weight and defensive groups advanced — rotation, not exit.

Asia-Pacific

Asia was mixed, with one sharp outlier:

  • South Korea KOSPI: 6,384.24, -3.65% — a fourth straight decline; Samsung Electronics and SK hynix fell more than 4% on foreign selling, AI profitability questions and the same memory-competition fears that hit U.S. chips
  • Australia S&P/ASX 200: 8,783.20, +0.76% — the region's leader
  • Hong Kong Hang Seng: 24,271.20, +0.25% (+60 points) — banks offset mixed tech; Xiaomi fell 4.9%, Orient Overseas rose 4.0%
  • China Shanghai Composite: 3,797.24, -0.43%
  • India Sensex: 71,924.68, -0.76%; Nifty 50 -0.70%
  • Japan Nikkei 225: essentially flat at about 69,031 — Japanese cash markets were largely closed for the Sports Day holiday

Europe, for context, was broadly higher: FTSE 100 +0.99% to 10,656.28, DAX +0.76% to 25,277.68, CAC 40 +0.38% to 7,832.88.

U.S. futures overnight

Futures were little changed Monday night as traders braced for bank earnings: Dow futures rose about 22 points (+0.05%), while S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 futures hovered around the flatline (CNBC live updates via GhanaMMA). One after-hours snapshot had S&P 500 futures -0.1% and Nasdaq-100 futures -0.2% — directionless either way, with the open set to be decided by earnings, not futures.

After-hours movers included Syndax Pharmaceuticals -16.3% after a Phase 2 trial miss (partner Incyte -3.9%), against gainers Biomea Fusion +6.6% and Navitas Semiconductor +4.7%.

Next-day outlook and economic calendar — Tuesday, October 13

Earnings before the open: JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo headline the first big day of Q3 season, alongside Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth, Domino's Pizza and Albertsons. Expectations are high — roughly 30% expected S&P 500 profit growth — so guidance and credit commentary matter as much as the beats.

The rest of the week: September CPI on Wednesday morning is the week's main event — the last major inflation release before the Fed's October 28 meeting — with the Beige Book Wednesday afternoon, then PPI and retail sales on Thursday. The bond market reopens Tuesday, restoring the yield signal that was missing Monday; if the 10-year pushes back toward 5.37%, Monday's quiet rotation could turn into something louder.
