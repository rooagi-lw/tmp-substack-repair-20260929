US Evening Report · Oct 14

Wednesday, October 14, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Market close

A hotter-than-expected September inflation report sent US stocks sharply lower on Wednesday, with the pain concentrated in the AI trade. The S&P 500 fell 1.11% (86.51 points) to 7,718.94 — its biggest one-day drop since July 29 — the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.88% (511.51 points) to 26,692.87, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped just 0.09% (48.62 points) to 51,621.48, per Reuters and the Associated Press closing reports. The Russell 2000 fell 1.93% to 2,729.54, as rate-sensitive small caps took the hardest hit alongside long-duration growth.

Breadth was less severe than the headline indexes suggested: at the close, 245 S&P 500 stocks had risen against 256 decliners, per Bullstory's closing snapshot — the cap-weighted loss was driven by the biggest technology names. Volume told the same story: QQQ turnover ran 48.5% above its 20-session average, while DIA volume was only 6.7% above average. It was the S&P 500's third straight decline from record highs, and the index now sits about 1% below its October 6 record close of 7,818.93.

Macro and policy

September CPI rose 0.6% month-on-month and 3.7% year-on-year, above the 3.6% consensus, with core CPI up 0.3% on the month and 2.6% on the year, also a tenth hotter than expected, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics via Reuters. Gasoline was the main driver — BLS/Reuters data put the monthly gasoline jump at 7.1% (9.1% on the AAA-based measure cited in market coverage) — but the firmer core reading made the report harder to dismiss as energy-only.

Rate-hike bets jumped. Interest-rate traders priced a 37% chance of a Fed hike at this month's meeting, up from 22% on Tuesday, per CME FedWatch via Reuters, and some coverage put the odds of rates being held in the current 3.75%–4.00% range at only about 49%, up sharply in the other direction before the print. Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin said the release was higher than he had hoped and kept the next decision open. The Fed's Beige Book, published Wednesday afternoon, said the effects of higher oil prices appeared in reports from all twelve districts, with businesses passing diesel and fuel-surcharge costs through supply chains, per the Seoul Economic Daily's summary of the report.

Oil kept the pressure on. WTI crude settled up 1.01% at $95.09 a barrel and Brent rose 0.35% to $106.06, per eOption/Hammerstone, as shipping attacks in and around the Strait of Hormuz kept a supply premium in the market — Hormuz crude exports averaged about 8.5 million barrels a day in the week to October 12, nearly 40% below prewar levels, per shipping data cited by CNBC. MBA mortgage applications fell 2.7% in the October 9 week, with the average 30-year mortgage rate at 7.50%, its highest since November 2023.

Sector performance

Eight of the eleven S&P 500 sectors declined, per Reuters. Information Technology was the weakest sector, down 2.6% on the Reuters sector measure (the XLK ETF fell about 3.0%), and Communication Services lost 1.78%, dragged by Meta's 3.27% slide. Financials fell as bank earnings landed badly — Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo and State Street were all notable decliners. Consumer Discretionary fell with Tesla down 2.15% and Amazon down 1.74%. Industrials fell as AI-adjacent power and equipment names (Caterpillar, Eaton, Vertiv, Quanta) tumbled with the data-center trade. Materials fell as gold and silver miners slid with bullion. Real Estate and Utilities fell as the day's rate shock hit dividend-paying and rate-sensitive stocks, and homebuilders touched an 18-month low.

The three gainers were the defensives and energy. Health Care led, helped by UnitedHealth and Amgen cushioning the Dow and by Eli Lilly's rise, and Consumer Staples rose on the defensive bid (both sectors were up around 2% on the day on eOption's closing review; an equal-weight snapshot put staples up 1.94% and health care up 1.37%). Energy rose with crude, and Exxon Mobil, Chevron and ConocoPhillips all touched record intraday highs before pulling back, per CNBC.

Top movers

  • The AI infrastructure complex was routed. CoreWeave (CRWV) tumbled about 15% as its bond yields spiking toward 15% spooked equity holders, Nebius (NBIS) fell about 10%, and Oracle (ORCL) lost 8.8%, per Reuters. Dell (DELL) sank 11.4% and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) fell 8.2% after Barclays downgraded both AI server makers to equal-weight.
  • Chips fell for a sixth straight session. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 4.3% — its longest losing streak since May 2025 — with AMD down 5.73%, Micron down 4.08%, TSMC down 3.40%, Broadcom down 3.10% and Nvidia down 3.16%, per Reuters and the Seoul Economic Daily. ASML beat on earnings and raised its outlook, yet its US shares still finished about 3.9% lower near $1,650.
  • Bank of America (BAC) fell 4.8% to $51.39 after a $1.2 billion FinCEN regulatory fine cut reported Q3 EPS to $0.91 ($1.08 adjusted) and revenue of about $30.0 billion missed expectations, per company filings and Dow Jones coverage. Morgan Stanley (MS) fell 2.75% to $181.51 despite beating with EPS of $3.03 on $20.34 billion of revenue and record wealth-management inflows, as fixed-income trading revenue dropped. BlackRock (BLK) was the bright spot, closing up 0.72% at $1,073.79 after adjusted EPS of $15.93 beat consensus and AUM rose 15% to $15.49 trillion.
  • Apple (AAPL) rose 0.5% and closed with a market capitalization above $5 trillion for the first time, per Reuters — the lone gainer among the mega-cap tech names.
  • Defensive and deal-driven gainers: Eli Lilly (LLY) rose about 3.3% to $1,205.24 on pipeline momentum; Liquidia (LQDA) jumped after its global settlement with United Therapeutics cleared its commercial path; GlobalFoundries (GFS) jumped after being selected to build and operate two New York fabs under a US–Japan initiative providing up to $21 billion in funding. Fastenal (FAST) fell despite beating on EPS, after guiding to heavier capital spending.

Technicals and sentiment

The VIX jumped about 10.5% to 16.87, its highest in two weeks — a real rise in hedging demand, but still well below panic levels, consistent with an orderly, rates-driven selloff rather than capitulation.

Treasury yields whipsawed. The 10-year yield spiked above 5.32% right after the CPI print, then rallied back to close at 5.19%, down about 2–3 basis points on the day (official daily par yield), per the WSJ and closing snapshots. The 2-year ended near 4.82%, after briefly trading above 4.92%, and the 30-year eased to about 5.53%. The dollar index rose about 0.4% to 102.54.

Precious metals were hit by the stronger dollar and the hike repricing: December gold settled down 1.66% at $4,107.90 an ounce and silver fell 6.13% to $55.88, per eOption/Hammerstone. Bitcoin declined about 2.0% to roughly $81,704.

Asia-Pacific markets

Asia-Pacific closed mostly lower on Wednesday, before the US inflation print. Japan's Nikkei 225 slipped 0.05% to 67,744.29, South Korea's KOSPI fell 0.44% to 6,008.81 — holding just above 6,000 after Tuesday's 5.5% plunge — Hong Kong's Hang Seng eased 0.13% to 24,314.17 (Hang Seng Tech rose 0.9%), mainland China's Shanghai Composite fell 0.88% to 3,767.23 with the CSI 300 down 1.4%, Taiwan's Taiex gained 0.6%, Australia's ASX 200 fell 0.64% to 8,727.90, and India's Sensex closed down 0.34% at 72,781.89, per SKN, ZeroHedge's Asia wrap and The Hindu BusinessLine.

China's data was the regional story: September's trade surplus hit a record $133.53 billion, with exports up 36.3% year-on-year (vs 25.5% expected) and imports up 31%, while CPI rose a softer-than-expected 0.9% year-on-year and PPI accelerated to 5.0%, per Newsquawk.

US futures overnight

Overnight futures extended the slide in early Thursday Asia trading: S&P 500 futures fell about 0.3%, Nasdaq-100 futures dropped about 0.9%, and Dow futures were flat, per TradingView/TE News — a snapshot, not a close, with investors waiting on Thursday's PPI, retail sales and TSMC earnings before adding risk back.

Next-day outlook and economic calendar

Thursday, October 15 — all at 8:30 AM ET: September PPI (consensus +0.5% month-on-month), September retail sales (consensus about +0.3% month-on-month, after +1.2% in August), weekly initial jobless claims (consensus about 198.5K, after 197K), the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index (consensus about 36.5, after 37.8) and the Empire State survey, per Morningstar. August business inventories follow at 10:00 AM ET.

Earnings: Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) is the day's key print — the next test of AI chip demand and CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity — alongside Charles Schwab, US Bancorp, PNC, Truist and Regions Financial, per Morningstar. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack speaks after the close, and Fed Chair Warsh is also on the calendar late in the day on some schedules. Friday brings September industrial production and import prices, and the Fed's pre-meeting blackout begins Saturday, October 17.

The question for Thursday is whether PPI confirms the CPI shock. A hot pipeline print on top of firm retail sales would keep an October hike live and pressure the AI trade further; a soft PPI would let yields retrace and give the dip-buyers their first real opening of the week.
