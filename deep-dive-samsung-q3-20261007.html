Samsung just posted the largest quarterly profit in South Korean corporate history — and it wasn't close.

What happened

  • This morning (Seoul time), Samsung's regulatory filing showed preliminary third-quarter operating profit of 107.4 trillion won (~$80.2 billion), according to Reuters — ahead of the LSEG SmartEstimate of 106.1 trillion won compiled from 21 analysts.
  • That's roughly 9x the 12.17 trillion won of operating profit from Q3 2025, and Samsung's fourth consecutive record quarter.
  • It makes Samsung the first South Korean company ever to top 100 trillion won in quarterly operating profit.
  • Revenue is estimated at 195 trillion won, up 127% year over year, per the filing.
  • The detailed breakdown by business division lands later in October — today's release is just the two headline numbers.

How we got here

  • 💾 HBM4 first-mover advantage. Samsung became the world's first to mass-produce and ship sixth-generation HBM4 in February, per Counterpoint Research. Its HBM revenue share climbed to roughly 33% in Q2 — up sharply — while longtime leader SK hynix slipped to 50% from 64% a year ago.
  • 🏗️ The AI capex supercycle. Hyperscaler AI data-center buildout keeps demand ahead of supply growth. Gartner projects global memory revenue jumping from $220 billion last year to $837 billion this year, then $1.075 trillion in 2027.
  • 📈 Pricing power. HBM4 costs for Nvidia GPUs run $31–32 per GB — nearly double HBM3E's $17–18, per Fubon Research via MoneyToday. Samsung told investors in July that Q3 HBM4 revenue would more than triple quarter over quarter.
  • 🔒 De-risking the cycle. In July Samsung said it aims to lock about two-thirds of its memory output into long-term agreements, per TrendForce — trading some upside for protection against the industry's historic boom-and-bust.
  • 🇺🇸 Confirmation from Micron. Micron's record results on September 30 came with a forecast that memory supply could be tighter in 2027 and 2028 than this year — validating the durability story Samsung is riding.

By the numbers

  • The chip division is the company now: Samsung's Device Solutions division earned 89.2 trillion of the 89.5 trillion won in Q2 operating profit, per europesays' tally — virtually 100%.
  • Memory operating margin is expected at ~76% in Q3, flat with Q2, per SK Securities analyst Han Dong-hee.
  • Combined, Samsung and SK hynix are on track for roughly 190 trillion won in Q3 operating profit — about 28% of South Korea's entire national budget, per BigGo Finance.
  • The 9x headline flatters on a weak base: Q3 2025's 12.17 trillion won was a trough quarter.
  • Currency was a real headwind: the won strengthened 14.3% in Q3, its biggest quarterly gain since 1998 — analysts cut consensus nearly 8% since end-August largely on FX.
  • Not everything is booming: the mobile (MX) division is expected to post a loss, per SamMobile, as soaring memory prices raise the cost of the phones Samsung itself sells.

Why it matters

This is the AI trade's purest distillation: memory scarcity converted into pricing power, converted into the biggest quarterly profit Korea has ever seen. The read-through is immediate — Micron jumped 4.1% to $1,088 on Wednesday after D.A. Davidson raised its price target to $3,000, and SK hynix reports October 27 into the same tailwind. More strategically, Samsung is closing the HBM gap with SK hynix faster than anyone expected a year ago, reshaping the competitive order of the most important input to AI infrastructure.

Bull vs. bear

The bull case: The HBM4 ramp is still early — HBM4 is expected to exceed 60% of Samsung's HBM sales in the second half, per TrendForce via Yonhap, and forecasts see Samsung potentially overtaking SK hynix in HBM share by 2027. Daishin Securities' Lee Kyung-min argues the guidance likely meets or beats the lowered expectations, and upward revisions could restore semiconductors' market leadership and push the KOSPI through 7,000.

The bear case: The pace of DRAM and NAND price gains slowed in Q3 — TrendForce sees Q4 conventional DRAM contract prices up only 10–15% versus ~60% in Q2 — fanning concern that chip margins have peaked. Bernstein recently cut its SK hynix target on HBM4 delivery issues and argues HBM could be less profitable than conventional DRAM by 2027. Add won appreciation, ceiling-priced long-term agreements capping upside, and Chinese rivals like CXMT winning OEM adoption, and the easy part of the cycle may be behind us.

What to watch next

  • Detailed Q3 results, late October — the division split: DS/memory margin, the MX loss size, foundry progress.
  • SK hynix Q3, October 27 — does the rival confirm the cycle, or show margin pressure first?
  • Q4 DRAM contract pricing — the TrendForce +10–15% call vs. the Q2 +60% surge.
  • HBM4 ramp at Nvidia's Vera Rubin and AMD's Instinct MI455X — the two design wins that decide Samsung's HBM trajectory.
  • OCP Summit (Oct 12–15) and SEDEX (Oct 14–16), plus ASML and TSMC earnings mid-October.

Samsung's $80 billion quarter is the AI boom at full throttle — but flat margins, slowing price gains, and a phone division losing money on expensive memory are the first hints that the supercycle's easy innings are ending.
