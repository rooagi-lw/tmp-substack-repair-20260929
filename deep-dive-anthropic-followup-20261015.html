Sixteen days after our first Anthropic deep dive — built on Reuters' look inside the company's confidential IPO prospectus — Anthropic showed prospective investors a new set of numbers, and they reset the scale of the story. At a closed pre-IPO investor day at its San Francisco headquarters on Wednesday, October 14, the company said third-quarter 2026 revenue was $16.8 billion, up 45% from the second quarter, and that it was profitable on an adjusted basis for the second straight quarter, with positive free cash flow (Financial Times, reported first; Reuters, from an attendee; Bloomberg, from people familiar). One label matters before anything else: these figures were stated to investors in a closed meeting. They are not from a filing, they are not audited, and Anthropic did not respond to Reuters' request for comment.

The new numbers

The quarterly series Anthropic laid out for investors:

  • Q1 2026: $4.7 billion
  • Q2 2026: $11.6 billion
  • Q3 2026: $16.8 billion

The three quarters sum to about $33.1 billion of recognized revenue in nine months. Set that against the September baseline from the draft prospectus: Anthropic's revenue for all of fiscal 2025 was $4.59 billion. The third quarter of 2026 alone is 3.7 times the entire prior year; the nine months are 7.2 times it.

Profitability flipped with the scale — on an adjusted basis. Anthropic reported a second consecutive quarter of adjusted profit, positive free cash flow (no amount given), an adjusted gross margin of 85%, and an adjusted operating margin above 11% (Bloomberg; The Information framed the 11% as year-to-date through Q3 excluding stock compensation — the period definition is unresolved). Executives put revenue at roughly $1.70 per $1 of compute in Q3. In fiscal 2025, the same ratio ran the other way: about $1.60 of compute for every $1 of revenue. What the adjusted figures exclude — reportedly training costs and stock-based compensation, in amounts Anthropic has not disclosed — is not public, and no GAAP profit or loss for any 2026 quarter was shared.

The deceleration inside the headline

The growth rate, not the level, is what the market seized on. Sequential growth went from about +147% in Q2 to +45% in Q3, and the dollar additions shrank too — roughly +$6.9 billion in Q2, then +$5.2 billion in Q3. Annualize the third quarter mechanically ($16.8 billion × 4) and you get about $67.2 billion — almost exactly the company's last run-rate marker, above $65 billion at the end of July (Reuters, August 17). In other words, on the simplest math, the annualized pace barely moved in a quarter when the stock-market narrative assumed it would sprint.

Anthropic pointedly did not settle the question: the FT reported the company gave no updated run-rate figure and no full-year 2026 guidance at the meeting. Into that vacuum, two very different numbers are circulating:

  • About $74 billion annualized — SemiAnalysis' estimate, cited by the FT.
  • About $110 billion by year-end — an expectation voiced by investors at the meeting (Wall Street Journal). Reaching it would require roughly $9.2 billion of revenue in December alone, against a third-quarter monthly average of about $5.6 billion — growth would have to re-accelerate, hard.

An extrapolation of the slower recent monthly pace lands in between, near $89 billion. None of these is company guidance; all three are estimates or expectations, and the spread between them is, in one line, the IPO pricing argument.

What has not changed

Almost everything else from the September prospectus reporting stands unrevised:

  • The commitments. At least $518 billion in future cloud, compute and infrastructure obligations, roughly 80% of it non-cancelable — including Google (at least $111.1 billion), Amazon ($110 billion), Microsoft ($31.4 billion), Broadcom-related leases ($161.2 billion) and xAI (up to $84.5 billion). No revision was reported this week.
  • The losses on a GAAP basis. Unknown for 2026. Fiscal 2025 showed an $8.06 billion operating loss and a net loss near $42 billion, about $34 billion of it a non-cash charge. Adjusted profit is not a substitute for the audited figures the public filing will have to show.
  • The cash position. $20.28 billion at December 31, 2025, before the reported $65 billion Series H. No updated balance was disclosed; positive third-quarter free cash flow was claimed without an amount.
  • The revenue mix. No new split of API, subscriptions, enterprise or Claude Code revenue was given. Secondary coverage credits Claude Code demand with much of Q2's surge, but there is no dollar figure. The September picture stands: nearly half of 2025 sales were routed through Amazon and Google, partners take about 16% of each cloud-partner dollar, two customers accounted for about 24% of revenue, and government was under 1%.
  • The accounting asymmetry. Anthropic books cloud-marketplace sales gross, as principal; OpenAI books its share net. Any side-by-side revenue comparison that ignores this — including some of the run-rate comparisons above — is not like-for-like.

The race with OpenAI

On the one clean comparison available, Anthropic is ahead: recognized revenue in Q2 2026 was $11.6 billion for Anthropic against $6.7 billion for OpenAI (Reuters, October 8–9) — the first quarter Anthropic has eclipsed its rival. OpenAI's annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at the end of September, per documents shared with investors (FT, October 8, via Reuters, which could not independently verify them); the roughly $70 billion figure circulated in late September turned out to be an investor-built gross-up designed to match Anthropic's counting method — an accounting gap, not a sales collapse. OpenAI is reported to be targeting a $70 billion-plus run rate by year-end, with its own IPO now expected in early 2027, after Anthropic's.

The forward look

The one substantial company-side projection in circulation is for 2028: revenue of $190–200 billion (Reuters, August 14, 2026, reporting an internal/banker projection). It was not reaffirmed at the investor day. For 2027, no current, credible company projection exists — internal figures reported in January 2026 (about $55 billion for 2027) have already been overtaken by events, with $33.1 billion recognized in the first nine months of 2026 alone. Reaching the 2028 number requires roughly tripling the current ~$67 billion annualized pace in a little over two years; at Q3's +45% quarterly growth the company would overshoot it, and at the slower monthly pace implied by August–September it would risk undershooting even the nearer-term run-rate talk. That tension is the story of the next twelve months.

What the valuation asks investors to believe, in multiples (our arithmetic):

  • At $2 trillion — the IPO target still cited by Reuters on October 14 — the price is about 436× fiscal 2025 revenue, ~30× annualized Q3 revenue, ~18× the $110 billion year-end run-rate hope, and ~10× the 2028 projected revenue. The ask is priced off 2028.
  • Investors at the meeting put fair value around $1.8–2 trillion beforehand (Bloomberg). After it, some reportedly expect bankers to trim the talk to below $1.5 trillion (The Information — unconfirmed). At $1.5 trillion, the multiple on annualized Q3 revenue works out to about 22×.
  • Analysts quoted by Bloomberg on October 15 split the difference: Saxo's Charu Chanana said the numbers strengthen the growth story while valuation remains the debate; UBP's Vey-Sern Ling expects a successful IPO possibly at a lower valuation, with slowing lab monetization a negative read-through for chip suppliers; Allspring's Gary Tan said the focus now shifts to revenue quality and whether the compute commitments earn adequate returns.

The IPO: timeline and structure

Nothing about the offering is company-confirmed beyond one fact: Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft S-1 on June 1, 2026 — the only fully official item in this entire story. As of October 15 there is no public S-1, no price range, no share count, no confirmed ticker and no lockup terms. What is reported:

  • Public S-1: SEC practice requires a public filing at least 15 days before a roadshow. If roadshow reporting holds, expect the filing around October 23–25 — it is the single biggest verification event ahead, with audited interim financials and the GAAP reconciliation of "adjusted profit."
  • Roadshow: the week of November 9 (Bloomberg, October 1).
  • Listing: sequenced after the November 3 midterms, with trading hoped for before Thanksgiving (November 26); Nasdaq is the reported exchange (Bloomberg, September). "ANTH" appears only as a proposed label in secondary trackers — it is not an official ticker.
  • Banks: Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley are reported as lead advisers (The Banker, October 2). A raise of up to $100 billion has been discussed in reporting but is unconfirmed.
  • Structure (from the draft prospectus, via Reuters): a Delaware public benefit corporation in which a Founder LLC — the seven co-founders holding roughly 2% each — directs a single Class F share carrying 50.1% of voting power, sunsetting only when two or fewer founders remain, while a Long-Term Benefit Trust appoints a majority of the board. Public shareholders get one vote per share. The draft filing itself warns this structure could produce decisions conflicting with public holders' financial interests.

The risks that could move it

  • Opacity: adjusted profit without a GAAP reconciliation, and no disclosure of what the adjustments exclude or by how much.
  • The commitment load: $518 billion, mostly non-cancelable, owed largely to the same Big Tech firms that are simultaneously Anthropic's investors, suppliers, distributors and competitors — firms that routed about half of 2025 revenue.
  • Concentration: two customers at about 24% of revenue; many large clients not on long-term contracts; distributor-routed growth that depends on partners' own model strategies.
  • Deflation: Reuters Breakingviews (October 15) argues token prices keep falling, so volume must grow exponentially just to hold the revenue path — with cheaper open-weight and Chinese models compounding the pressure.
  • Politics and safety scrutiny: on October 15, New York City Comptroller Mark Levine publicly urged Anthropic to pause the IPO over safety and governance concerns; a reported FTC industry probe and the prospectus' own warnings about catastrophic-risk scenarios sit in the background.
  • Market weather: the numbers landed in a hot-inflation, higher-yield week in which AI infrastructure stocks sold off hard — Barron's noted AMD, Micron, SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital down around 5% early Thursday, though the rout had several drivers beyond Anthropic.

What to watch

  • ~October 23–25: the public S-1 window, if the November roadshow is real.
  • November 3: US midterms, after which the listing is reported to be sequenced.
  • Week of November 9: the reported roadshow.
  • Before November 26: the hoped-for trading debut.
  • Q4 evidence: any December run-rate print against the $74B / $89B / $110B spread, and whether growth re-accelerates from +45%.
  • OpenAI's year-end print against its $70 billion-plus run-rate ambition — the direct comp for Anthropic's pricing.

Bottom line

Anthropic's third quarter — $16.8 billion of revenue, a second straight adjusted profit, positive cash flow — would be a triumphal headline at almost any other company. At a $2 trillion asking price it instead sharpens the only question that matters: whether growth re-accelerates enough to justify pricing the IPO off 2028. The company declined to update the one number that would answer it. The public S-1, due within roughly ten days if the reported timeline holds, is where the audited version of this story finally has to appear.
