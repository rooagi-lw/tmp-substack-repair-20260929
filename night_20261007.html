US Evening Report · Oct 7

Wednesday, October 7, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Market close

US stocks retreated Wednesday in their first losing session of October, as Treasury yields re-tested multi-decade highs and pared losses only after a well-received 10-year note auction. The S&P 500 fell 0.22% (17.16 points) to 7,801.77, one day after its record close above 7,800, per the WSJ citing Dow Jones Market Data. The Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.22% (61.20 points) to 27,538.69. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 0.66% (341.41 points) to 51,179.87, after trading as low as 50,917 intraday, per IBD. Small caps bore the brunt: the Russell 2000 dropped 1.3% to 2,793.20, roughly 9% below its record highs from earlier this year and nearing correction territory, per the WSJ. The pullback snapped a four-day winning streak for the S&P 500 and a five-day run for the Nasdaq, per Barron's.

Breadth was poor: about 73% of S&P 500 components declined (369 of 500, per FactSet via MarketWatch), and 52-week lows outnumbered highs 506 to 48.

Tuesday's closes for reference: S&P 500 7,818.93 (+0.58%, record), Nasdaq 27,599.79 (+0.45%, record), Dow 51,521.28 (+0.49%).

Macro and policy

Bond-market pressure set the tone. The 10-year Treasury yield rose a hair to 5.276% after touching 5.36% intraday — a 24-year high, highest since 2002, per the WSJ. The 30-year rose to 5.66%, on the verge of its highest since 2002, while the 2-year eased to 4.762%. The $39 billion 10-year reopening auction drew strong demand — the notes sold at a 5.30% yield, 0.018 percentage points below the when-issued level, with foreign investors active — helping yields pull back from the day's highs, per Barron's and Reuters.

The September FOMC minutes (from the Sept. 15–16 meeting that lifted rates to 3.75%–4.00%) showed a united vote but a divided rationale: some officials saw the hike as insurance against energy-driven price pressure and inflation expectations, others as the opening move against demand-driven inflation. Most officials anticipated another hike this year, the WSJ reported. CME FedWatch puts October hold odds near 83% (a December hike remains roughly 83% priced), per Barron's. The minutes also revealed it was the US Treasury — not the Fed — that carried out America's part in Japan's late-July yen intervention.

Rising yields are biting housing: the average 30-year fixed mortgage hit 7.52%, near three-year highs, per Bankrate via the WSJ; the SPDR S&P Homebuilders ETF (XHB) fell 2.6% and is down more than 8% year to date.

Geopolitics kept oil elevated. Brent traded near $101–102 and WTI slipped about 1.3% to roughly $88.30, per Reuters, after fresh attacks on Saudi airports and tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. The dollar index rose 0.4% to 102.45, a level last seen in April 2025, per tradingnews.com.

Abroad, India's central bank raised its repo rate 25 bps to 5.50% — its first hike in nearly four years — shifting its stance to calibrated tightening; the Sensex fell 0.6% and India VIX rose 2% to 13.88, per the Hindu BusinessLine and hdfcsky.

And the AI capex arms race turned to credit markets: the FT reported SpaceX is in talks to raise $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips, and the WSJ added that Broadcom is seeking about $50 billion in financing with Oracle also raising money for AI chips. SpaceX credit-default insurance hit record highs.

Sector performance

Seven of the 11 GICS sectors finished lower, per Reuters. Health Care was the best, up 1.1%, while Industrials was the worst, down 2.1%, per Barron's. Health care's strength came from large-cap biotech and pharma — AbbVie, Amgen and Moderna all gained. Industrials were dragged down by Caterpillar's 6% slide on continued regulatory pressure. Semiconductors were a soft spot within tech (the SMH/SOXX funds fell about 1%), while rate-sensitive real estate and homebuilders lagged as mortgage rates climbed.

Top movers

  • Levi Strauss (LEVI) fell 5% in the regular session to $19.51 ahead of its earnings report, and dropped another 2%+ after hours: Q3 adjusted EPS of $0.48 beat the $0.36 consensus, but $79 million of the profit came from IEEPA tariff refunds; revenue rose 4% to $1.61 billion, a touch below estimates; management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $1.54–1.56 but US direct-to-consumer sales were soft, per Barron's and Reuters. Women's denim remains the bright spot.
  • NRG Energy (NRG) jumped 4.8%, Barron's "Hot Stock" of the day, and Vistra gained more than 4%, as the AI power-buildout trade continued after Tuesday's Constellation–Alphabet deal.
  • Moderna (MRNA) rallied 5%, and Micron (MU) gained 4%, alongside strength in memory names Sandisk (~+4%) and biotech, per Reuters and MarketWatch.
  • Constellation Energy (CEG) gave back just 0.3% to $299.59, holding most of Tuesday's 12%+ surge, per IBD.
  • Caterpillar (CAT) sank 6% and SpaceX (SPCX) dropped 2.6% on the chip-financing reports, per Reuters; Skydance fell 6.7%, Barron's biggest loser of the day.
  • Constellation Brands (STZ) tumbled about 7% in early trading after Tuesday's earnings report, while Neogen (NEOG) soared more than 14% on its results, per IBD.
  • Webull (BULL) plunged about 20% intraday on a House China report, per tradingnews.com; Seagate extended Tuesday's 9% slide and Western Digital fell below its 200-day line on HDD pricing concerns, per IBD.

Technicals and sentiment

The VIX rose roughly 5% to the high-15s late in the session (around 15.7–15.8, per MarketWatch intraday quotes), up from Tuesday's 15.06 close — calm by historical standards, with the equity-volatility gauge near its 2026 lows even as bond volatility stays elevated.

The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.276%, up a fraction of a basis point after touching a 24-year intraday high, per the WSJ and IBD.

Gold spot fell $61.42 (-1.5%) to about $4,102 an ounce — a two-month low — as surging yields and a stronger dollar outweighed Middle East risk, per tradingnews.com. COMEX gold futures lost 1.4% and silver futures dropped 2.5% to $60.04.

Asia-Pacific overnight

Asia's Wednesday session (local October 7) finished mostly lower, failing to follow Wall Street's record Tuesday. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.92% to 70,035.71, holding just above 70,000 as investors booked chip profits and beer makers slid on a Fair Trade Commission price-fixing probe; the Topix lost 0.5%. Korea's KOSPI dropped 1.98% to 6,803.90, with foreigners net selling for an eighth session and SK Hynix (-2.8%) weak ahead of Samsung's preliminary results Thursday. Hong Kong's Hang Seng slipped 0.62% to 24,130.50 on tech weakness (Alibaba -2.6%, Tencent -1.8%). Taiwan's Taiex eased 0.03%, Singapore's STI fell 1.6%, and Australia's ASX 200 was about flat. India's Sensex closed 0.59% lower at 72,638.70 after the RBI's rate hike. Mainland China was closed for the Golden Week holiday and reopens Thursday.

Early Thursday trading in Asia is lower again: the Nikkei down about 1%, the KOSPI off 0.6%, and Hang Seng futures pointing to a 0.6% lower open, per Reuters and CNBC.

US futures overnight

S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures were barely changed late Wednesday night, per Reuters, with European futures up a fraction after sliding Wednesday.
