Homebuilders -1.6% — the bond rout is back, and housing is taking the hit.

Why it's moving

🏦 The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.33% early Thursday — its highest since April 2002, according to CoinCentral's pre-market update. Mortgage rates track the 10-year, and buyers are feeling it.

📉 Fed's Neel Kashkari warned on ongoing inflation risks overnight, adding to fears the central bank stays hawkish. The bond market just completed its worst quarter this century — yields up 87 bps in Q3 (eOption).

🏠 The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate sits at 7.12%, the highest since May 2024, and new-home mortgage applications fell 1.5% week over week, per Mortgage Bankers Association data reported by MarketWatch.

🌍 It's global: UK 30-year gilt yields touched ~6%, highest since 1998, and British housebuilder stocks fell 2.9% on Thursday (Reuters).

Driving it

Pure homebuilder bloodletting. The Big Three are all down around 3%:

  • DHI (D.R. Horton) -2.9% to $132.65
  • LEN (Lennar) -2.9% to $79.22
  • PHM (PulteGroup) -2.7% to $112.88

Home-furnishing names are weak too — Wayfair was XHB's biggest decliner Wednesday at -8.3% (MarketWatch).

Technicals

PHM -2.7% — below 20-day ($119.85) and 50-day ($125.54); broke the 3-month low $114.78 intraday, next support $108.49 (52-week low), resistance $116.06 (prior close); hourly downtrend, 15-min steady bleed all session.

Elsewhere

Energy +1.3% as Brent topped $100/bbl after China suspended oil product exports. Materials -1.5% joined the yield-driven selloff.

Takeaway

Rates are the whole story — until the 10-year finds a ceiling, builders stay uninvestable.
