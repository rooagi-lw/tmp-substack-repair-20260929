US Evening Report · Oct 16

Friday, October 16, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Wall Street ended a brutal week for the AI trade with a split decision: technology was sold hard, oil topped $100, and six of the eleven S&P 500 sectors still managed to rise. The question hanging over markets is no longer whether AI spending is real, but whether it can be financed at 5%-plus Treasury yields while crude above $100 keeps inflation — and the Federal Reserve — on edge.

The close

  • S&P 500: 7,659.44, down 84.62 points (-1.09%) — Wall Street Journal close via Market Briefer
  • Nasdaq Composite: 26,140.03, down 572.99 points (-2.14%) — Wall Street Journal close via Market Briefer
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 52,002.41, down 175.28 points (-0.34%), after rising more than 1% early in the session — Investopedia / Wall Street Journal
  • Russell 2000: down about 1.7% on the day — Briefing.com summary via Percy's Perspective

For the week, the Nasdaq fell about 4.5% — its biggest weekly decline since the week ended June 26, snapping a four-week winning streak (Investopedia). The S&P 500 fell about 2.0% for the week, while the Dow still rose about 0.7%, helped by banks and other non-tech names (Investopedia).

Why it moved: the AI trade's problem is now its financing

CoreWeave fell about 12% on Friday (Reuters via Market Briefer put the close at -11.91%) as its bonds yielded above 15% — investors are questioning debt-funded data-center expansion at the marginal AI borrower. Dell Technologies fell 4.6%, capping a weekly loss above 22%, its worst five days since its 2018 return to public markets (Market Briefer). The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 3.8% on Friday, leaving it down 11.4% for the week (Briefing.com via Percy's Perspective).

At the same time, oil took the day. WTI crude settled up $3.44 (+3.5%) at $101.41 a barrel — its first close above $100 since September 21 — and Brent rose 3.72% to $109.86, after renewed Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries, Strait of Hormuz shipping risk and a large U.S. inventory draw (Dow Jones Market Data via Market Briefer). Two squeezes are running at once: credit costs for leveraged AI builders, and energy costs for everyone else.

Macro & policy

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, speaking at an IMF event in Bangkok, declined to signal whether the Fed will hold or hike at its late-October meeting. Following his comments, traders saw a 17% likelihood of a rate hike at the late-October meeting, down from 28% the day before, and an 89% likelihood of a hike at the December meeting, unchanged (CME FedWatch via Investopedia). The Fed's pre-meeting blackout begins Saturday.

The day's data beat on growth and stayed hot on prices, per the Federal Reserve and Labor Department releases summarized by Market Briefer and Briefing.com: September industrial production rose 0.7% against 0.4% expected, manufacturing output rose 0.3%, and capacity utilization reached 76.9%. September import prices climbed 1.1% on the month and 8.1% from a year earlier — another inflation input after this week's hotter-than-expected CPI and PPI readings.

Six of eleven sectors rose (sector index closes compiled by Edge Consultancy's US Market Wrap, with Select Sector SPDR moves broadly in line):

  • Energy: +2.47% — the clear leader on crude's surge (XLE +2.52%, Finnhub)
  • Utilities: +1.40% (XLU +1.34%, Finnhub)
  • Consumer Staples: +1.31% (XLP +1.08%, Finnhub)
  • Materials: +0.68% (XLB +0.71%, Finnhub)
  • Financials: +0.49% — regional bank earnings from M&T Bank and Citizens Financial came in generally strong (Market Briefer); XLF +0.45%, Finnhub
  • Real Estate: +0.38% (XLRE +0.40%, Finnhub)
  • Industrials: -0.05% (XLI -0.08%, Finnhub)
  • Health Care: -0.26% (XLV -0.27%, Finnhub)
  • Consumer Discretionary: -1.17%
  • Communication Services: -2.45%
  • Information Technology: -2.54% — the drag; the Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) fell 3.02% (Finnhub)

Top movers

Losers were concentrated in AI, chips and software:

  • Twilio: -10.6%, the biggest fall in the S&P 500 on Friday (MarketWatch)
  • CoreWeave: -11.91% (Briefing.com via Percy's Perspective)
  • Super Micro Computer: -9.86%; Sandisk: -6.85%; AMD: -5.95%; Intel: -5.79%; Micron: -5.63% (Stockchase / Briefing.com)
  • Oracle: -5%; ServiceNow: -4%; Salesforce: -2% (MarketWatch via The Orbit Post); Palo Alto Networks and CrowdStrike each fell more than 7%
  • A broad software fund (iShares Expanded Tech-Software) closed down 4.8% on Friday and lost 7.4% for the week; a semiconductor fund (iShares Semiconductor) closed down 4.1% on Friday (MarketWatch / Investor's Business Daily)

On the upside:

  • Diamondback Energy: +4.27% on the oil surge (Stockchase)
  • Varonis Systems: +2.9% after Proofpoint agreed to acquire the data-security firm for about $6.3 billion (Market Briefer)
  • Apple: +0.43% to $343.46, bucking the tech selloff (Briefing.com via Percy's Perspective)

Unusual volume: trading ran above average — NYSE volume 1,396 million shares vs a 1,269 million average, and Nasdaq volume 9,600 million vs an 8,221 million average (Briefing.com internals via Percy's Perspective). Decliners led advancers by roughly 4-to-3 on the NYSE and nearly 3-to-1 on the Nasdaq by the close.

Technicals & sentiment

  • VIX: surged 22.5% to 20.16 on increased portfolio hedging (Briefing.com via Percy's Perspective) — back above 20 for the first time in this selloff
  • 10-year Treasury yield: 5.10%, up 1 basis point on the day but down 14 basis points for the week (Briefing.com via Percy's Perspective); the official Treasury par yield table put it at 5.09% (U.S. Treasury via Edge Consultancy)
  • 2-year yield: 4.74%, down 1 basis point
  • Technical damage: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite knifed through their 50-day moving averages this week, the small-cap Russell 2000 dived below its 200-day line, and the Dow rose but hit resistance at its 50-day line (Investor's Business Daily)
  • Dollar: the Dollar Index fell about 0.2% to 101.83, losing 0.4% for the week (Briefing.com)
  • Gold: December futures about $4,181 an ounce, up about 0.3% (settlement data via Edge Consultancy / Briefing.com)
  • Bitcoin: near $81,066, down 1.47% late in the day (CoinGecko feed via Market Briefer)

Asia-Pacific & Europe

Asia snapped its rout where it began (Market Briefer): Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 1.39% to 65,324.29 after six down sessions; South Korea's KOSPI rebounded 2.05% to 5,668.05, ending seven straight declines — though it still lost about 14% on the week, its biggest weekly fall since October 2008. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.52% to 24,186.83, the Shanghai Composite added 0.24% to 3,716.28, and Australia's All Ordinaries rose 1.1% (Briefing.com via Percy's Perspective). Europe closed its strongest week since late June: the STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.72% to 644.74, Germany's DAX gained 0.95%, and Britain's FTSE 100 added 0.38% (Market Briefer).

US futures overnight

Index futures do not trade over the weekend. Dow Jones, S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures reopen Sunday at 6:00 PM ET (Investor's Business Daily) — the first overnight read on whether Friday's AI and credit selling stabilizes will come then, not before.

Next-day outlook & economic calendar

Saturday brings no U.S. trading, and the Fed blackout begins. The next session is Monday, October 19:

  • Asia opens with China's third-quarter GDP plus September industrial output and retail sales — a direct read on demand for chip, metals and luxury exporters (Market Briefer)
  • Canada reports September CPI and the Bank of Canada's Business Outlook Survey (Market Briefer)
  • Earnings season then takes over: Netflix reports Tuesday, October 20 after the close (Morningstar), alongside GE Aerospace, General Motors, 3M, Capital One and Intuitive Surgical (MarketBeat); Meta, Tesla and IBM follow on Wednesday (Market Briefer / Investor's Business Daily)
  • Central banks ahead: the Fed decision in late October, then the ECB on October 29 and the Bank of Japan on October 30 (Market Briefer)

What would change the read: a decisive fall in the 10-year yield back below 5% while crude holds above $100, and Big Tech earnings that show whether AI profits can carry AI debt.
