Hot Sector: Semiconductors -3.7%

🔥 Semiconductors -3.7% as of 9:29 a.m. ET — the AI rout stretched toward an eighth straight down session for SOXX, even as TSMC posted record earnings: this selloff is about the cost of money, not chip demand.

Why it's moving

  • 🔥 September CPI came in at 3.7% versus 3.6% expected on Wednesday; market reports put odds of a Fed hike at the October 28 meeting near 44–49%, according to Reuters' market coverage.
  • 🇰🇷 South Korea's KOSPI crashed 7.57% to a six-month low after a circuit-breaker halt — Samsung Electronics -9.5% and SK Hynix -11.1%, per Reuters.
  • 🏭 TSMC reported a record Q3 — profit up 68.7% — and raised its Q4 guide, but struck a cautious note for 2027, per Reuters. TSM -3.6% anyway.
  • 💳 AI financing stress deepened: CoreWeave fell 4.5% premarket after a 15% drop Wednesday, with its bond yields near 15%; Oracle -2% premarket, per Reuters.
  • 🛡️ Money rotated defensive, not out: Consumer Staples (XLP) +1.9%, Healthcare (XLV) +1.4%, Utilities (XLU) +1.1%, and Dow futures were higher — a rotation, not a panic.

Driving it

Memory and foundry/logic lead the decline: AMD -5.4%, Micron (MU) -4.7%, TSMC (TSM) -3.6%, Nvidia (NVDA) -2.8%. Memory is hit hardest — Korean memory giants crashed overnight and China's CXMT memory progress, reported earlier this week, keeps pressure on the group, per Barron's.

Technicals

  • AMD -5.4% ($540.00) — far below its 20-day average (~$612), testing the 50-day (~$537); session low $538.52; hourly downtrend, 15-minute still making lower lows.
  • MU -4.7% ($871.36) — broke below Wednesday's low; well under the 20-day (~$1,039) and 50-day (~$983); support at today's low $863, resistance at $914.
  • TSM -3.6% ($414.40) — testing its 20-session low near $414; below the 50-day (~$434); resistance at Wednesday's close $430.
  • NVDA -2.8% ($215.21) — below the 20-day (~$229) and 50-day (~$223); support at today's low $214.65, resistance at $223.

Takeaway

Record chip earnings cannot outrun 3.7% inflation and 15% AI borrowing costs — until yields ease, every semiconductor bounce is being sold.
