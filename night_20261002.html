US Evening Report · Oct 2

Friday, October 2, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

US Market Close Recap

Wall Street ended the week on a strong note as a much weaker-than-expected September jobs report cooled bets that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again at its October meeting. The S&P 500 rose 0.73% to 7,722.72, the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 0.49% to 51,176.96, and the Nasdaq Composite surged 1.19% to 27,190.86 — its second-highest close on record, with a fresh intraday high along the way. The Nasdaq 100 gained 1% to 30,807.93, and the small-cap Russell 2000 added 0.94% to 2,832.90.

The weekly picture was more mixed: the Nasdaq rose 0.5%, its third straight weekly gain, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.3% and the Dow fell 1.3%.

Macro & Policy

The day's driver was the September nonfarm payrolls report: the economy added just 29,000 jobs, far below the roughly 84,000–90,000 economists expected, and August's gain was revised down to 133,000 from 162,000. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2% from 4.1%, and wage growth slowed as well.

For markets, weak is good right now: traders slashed the odds of an October rate hike to about 23–24%, down from 64% a week ago, according to CME FedWatch. That follows the Fed's September rate hike — its first since 2023 — which had left investors braced for more. Fed officials, including Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and New York Fed President John Williams, have signaled they have room to wait and evaluate data ahead of the October 27–28 FOMC meeting.

Earlier in the week, a cooler-than-expected August core PCE reading (3.0% year-on-year vs. 3.3% forecast) helped the case for patience, while an upward Q2 GDP revision (2.2% vs. 1.5%) and a hot ISM manufacturing prices-paid print pulled the other way.

Sector Performance

Breadth was healthy: 10 of the 11 S&P 500 sectors finished higher, with only Health Care in the red (down about 0.3%, per the Health Care Select Sector SPDR — and the week's worst performer at −2.9%). Consumer Discretionary led the day with a gain of more than 1%, followed by Materials and Technology, each up about 1%. Tech leadership came from semiconductors, riding the momentum of Micron's strong results midweek.

Top Movers

Chips stole the show. The PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) jumped 2.4% to its highest level in three months. Teradyne surged 8% to close at $449.04, Arm Holdings climbed 5.2% to $307.49, Macom Technology rose 6.6% to $321.60, and Nvidia hit a new all-time high, up 1.3% to $233.95; AMD also touched an intraday record. ON Semiconductor rose 6% to $84.89 after making a revised $5.7 billion cash bid for Synaptics, which itself jumped 14%. For the week, Lumentum (+15.3%) and Synopsys (+15.1%) led Nasdaq gainers.

Tesla was the mega-cap standout, up 4.7% to $370.59 after reporting 486,532 third-quarter deliveries — well above the roughly 463,000 consensus — plus 13.7 GWh of energy storage deployments.

On the losing side, Nike tumbled about 9% after forecasting a bigger full-year sales decline than expected, citing weakness in China and announcing job cuts. Seagate Technology and Western Digital both plunged about 10% after a Nikkei Asia report that Toshiba could double its hard-disk-drive supply.

Also notable: Moderna rose 2.3% on news it will replace Warner Bros. Discovery in the Nasdaq-100 index starting October 9, and Alphabet gained 1.6% after putting its TPU chip technology into orbit aboard a SpaceX rideshare mission.

Technicals & Sentiment

The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) fell 6% to 15.40, signaling a meaningful drop in near-term equity anxiety — though strategists noted the calm in stocks contrasts with rising volatility in the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to close at 5.276%, just below the 24-year high of about 5.34% touched on Thursday; the 30-year ended at 5.629% and the 2-year at 4.823%. Yields initially fell on the jobs report, then reversed higher as U.S.–Iran tensions kept energy-market risk alive.

In commodities, WTI crude fell 1.5% to $91.44 a barrel and Brent pulled back to about $101 as the EU discussed releasing diesel stockpiles, even as the U.S. reportedly moved a third aircraft carrier toward the Middle East. Gold eased 0.7% to $4,171.70 an ounce, and Bitcoin slipped to about $84,300.

Asia-Pacific Markets

A mixed Friday across the region. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index sank 2.6% to 23,972 — its biggest one-day drop since July and a two-and-a-half-month low — as financial heavyweights sold off; HSBC fell more than 5% and AIA nearly 6%. Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 0.9% to 68,309 after Tokyo core inflation accelerated to 2.7%, reinforcing Bank of Japan tightening bets. South Korea's KOSPI bucked the trend, rising 0.45% to reclaim 7,000 at 7,003.74, while Taiwan's TAIEX added 0.3% and Australia's ASX 200 gained 0.8% to 8,682. Mainland China stayed closed for the National Day Golden Week holiday (reopening October 8); India was closed for Gandhi Jayanti.

US Futures Overnight

U.S. equity futures are closed for the weekend and resume trading Sunday at 6:00 PM ET. Heading into the weekend, futures had pointed to Friday's strong open — Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 futures all finished the week higher. With the jobs report now absorbed, Sunday evening's reopen will be the first test of whether the "weak data, no hike" relief trade carries into next week.

Outlook & Economic Calendar

Next week is lighter on tier-one data, but it still matters. The last major inflation reading before the October 27–28 FOMC meeting arrives Wednesday, October 14 (September CPI). Until then:

  • Monday, Oct 5: S&P Global services PMI and ISM services index
  • Tuesday, Oct 6: August trade balance
  • Wednesday, Oct 7: Minutes from the Fed's September meeting, plus a 10-year note auction
  • Thursday, Oct 8: Weekly jobless claims and wholesale trade
  • Friday, Oct 9: University of Michigan preliminary consumer sentiment

Earnings pick up too: Delta Air Lines, PepsiCo, Constellation Brands, Levi's and Applied Digital all report, offering early reads on travel, the consumer and AI infrastructure demand.

The market's baseline is now a Fed on hold in October — but with the 10-year back above 5.25% and oil over $90, bond volatility remains the swing factor into year-end.
