Deep Dive: Constellation — Google's 20-Year Nuclear Bet

Google just signed the largest tech-to-nuclear power deal ever struck — a 20-year agreement that pays Constellation Energy to squeeze an entire reactor's worth of new capacity out of 11 existing plants.

What happened

  • ⚛️ The deal (Oct 6, 2026): Google and Constellation announced a 20-year power purchase agreement for 890 MW of new nuclear capacity — delivered through "uprates" (turbine, steam-generator and digital-control upgrades) at 11 CEG reactors in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey. First power lands in 2028; upgrade work runs through 2032/33 (per Reuters, WSJ, and the companies' joint release).
  • 💰 Constellation's bill: more than $4.3 billion of private investment in the plants — the largest uprate program ever agreed between a nuclear operator and a tech company, according to the Wall Street Journal.
  • ⚡ Plus baseload supply: a separate 15-year agreement for 2,700 MW from Constellation's broader fleet serving the PJM grid (the largest US grid operator, covering 13 states plus DC). Combined, the two agreements total 3,590 MW.
  • 🤖 The tech layer: a 5-year "AI for Energy" alliance puts Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise onto grid operations — the companies' stated aim is to use AI to cut new-infrastructure costs and keep generation ahead of demand (per the companies' statement, via Investopedia).
  • 📈 The market verdict: Constellation shares jumped ~15% to ~$308 in afternoon trading (per WSJ and Barron's); sympathy buying lifted Talen Energy +13%, Vistra +12% and NRG +9% (per WSJ).

How we got here

  • 2024: Microsoft signs a power deal tied to the Crane/Three Mile Island restart — electricity targeted for late 2027 — planting the flag on big-tech nuclear contracting.
  • 2026: Meta inks a 20-year deal for the Clinton plant starting June 2027; in January Constellation closes the Calpine acquisition, growing the fleet to roughly 55 GW — about 10% of US clean energy (per company disclosure).
  • Aug 6, 2026: Q2 results — Constellation signed 920 MW of new long-term PPAs in the quarter alone, including 176 MW with Walmart at Dresden (per the Q2 earnings call transcript, via Yahoo Finance).
  • Sep 30, 2026: Amazon signs a 20-year deal for 690 MW at Calvert Cliffs plus a 190 MW uprate — over $3 billion of investment (per Constellation's announcement).
  • Oct 6, 2026: Google — Constellation's second big-tech nuclear deal in a week, and the biggest yet.

By the numbers

  • Q2 2026 (reported Aug 6): revenue $7.50 billion, up 23% year over year (per the company) but below the ~$7.8–7.9 billion consensus (per Yahoo Finance and ainvest); adjusted operating EPS $2.55, up 34% (per the company), beating consensus of ~$2.28–2.41 (per MarketBeat and Edgen).
  • Guidance raised: full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance lifted to $11.50–$12.50 from $11.00–$12.00 (per the Yahoo Finance transcript of the Aug 6 earnings call).
  • GAAP noise: net income was $513 million, down 39% on non-cash mark-to-market and merger items — the adjusted number is the operational read (per AlphaStreet).
  • Operating proof: Q2 nuclear output ran at a ~93% capacity factor — roughly 40% better than the industry average — across a fleet that supplies about 10% of US clean energy (per the Q2 results and company disclosure).
  • Valuation reset: CEG entered Tuesday down 24% for 2026 on AI-capex anxiety (per Investopedia) and 37.6% below its $412.70 52-week high (per ad-hoc-news/stockanalysis.com) — today's move is a violent re-rating off a depressed base.

Why it matters

This deal rewrites the AI-power playbook. As the companies' own blog post put it (via MarketWatch), uprates deliver "substantial capacity gains without the multi-year timelines or interconnection bottlenecks associated with greenfield construction." Translation: tech doesn't need to wait for new reactors — it can buy contracted megawatts from upgrades to plants that already exist. For Constellation, a 20-year contracted buyer converts uncertain merchant power prices into a bond-like revenue stream, directly supporting the 20%+ base EPS growth narrative (per AlphaStreet). And the cadence — Amazon on Sep 30, Google on Oct 6 — suggests the pipeline of tech buyers is accelerating, not tapering.

Bull vs. bear

Bull (Motley Fool, Oct 1): Constellation "operates the most nuclear-energy-producing facilities in the U.S." — carbon-free, around-the-clock — "and Constellation is already there," making it the better long-term buy than NextEra.

Bear (Motley Fool, Sep 8): The contracted demand arrives in stages — Meta's Clinton power starts June 2027, Crane lands late 2027, the newest deals contribute in 2029–2032: "Not one of them adds anything to this year's results."

What to watch next

  • 2028: first deliveries from the Google uprate program — proof the model executes.
  • May 2027: the NRC operating-license decision on the Crane restart, with generation targeted late 2027 (per AlphaStreet).
  • Q3 results (~early November): Calpine integration progress and commercial execution.
  • The next signature: with Amazon and Google done in seven days, which hyperscaler — or which data-center developer — signs the third big PPA; and whether PJM finalizes its "Bring Your Own Power" proposal.

Takeaway

The AI power race just got its first proven playbook: don't build new reactors — squeeze 20 years of contracted megawatts out of the ones already running, and let the tech giants pay for it.
