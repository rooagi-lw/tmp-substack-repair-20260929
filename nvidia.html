📌 What happened

  • 💰 Nvidia announced a $150 billion addition to its share-repurchase authorization on Monday — the largest stock buyback ever announced by a U.S. company.

  • 📊 The move brings Nvidia's total buyback authorization to $235 billion, which the company expects to execute through fiscal 2028.

  • 🚀 Shares jumped +2.6% to ~$231 on the news (opened at $225.07), making Nvidia the lone gainer among megacap chip stocks while the SOXX semiconductor ETF fell -3.2%.

  • 🛢️ The buyback landed as the AI trade faced a macro test: Brent crude above $106 on renewed U.S.-Iran tensions revived inflation and rate fears, pressuring high-multiple tech.

📊 Why it matters

  • A $150B buyback at a $5.42 trillion market cap signals management sees value even after Nvidia's massive run — and puts a floor under the year's most crowded trade.

  • Nvidia has the cash flow to fund it: Q2 delivered $2.22 EPS vs $2.09 expected on $96.22B revenue (+105.9% y/y), with 63.7% net margins.

  • The divergence is the story: with AMD -4.8%, Intel -3.9%, and Broadcom -0.8%, Nvidia is single-handedly keeping the AI-infrastructure narrative alive.

Takeaway: When the macro turned against chips, Nvidia answered with the biggest buyback in U.S. history — $150B says the AI buildout isn't done.
