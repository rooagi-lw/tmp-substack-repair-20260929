US Evening Report · Oct 15

Thursday, October 15, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

US stocks staged a broad reversal on Thursday. The Dow jumped more than 550 points as money rotated out of the AI infrastructure trade and into health care, staples, financials and consumer names — even after the hottest wholesale inflation print in four and a half years. Falling Treasury yields, not soft data, did the heavy lifting.

Market close

The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1.08% (557.51 points) to 52,178.99, the S&P 500 gained 0.33% (25.18 points) to 7,744.12, and the Nasdaq Composite edged up 0.08% (20.16 points) to 26,713.03, per Jin10 closing data via Binance and eOption's closing recap. The Russell 2000 rose 1.38% to about 2,767, making small caps the day's strongest major benchmark.

The reversal was dramatic intraday. Nasdaq futures had fallen more than 1% overnight, the Nasdaq neared a one-month low in the morning, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was down more than 4% at its worst before closing down just 0.88% — still its seventh straight decline. Eight of the eleven S&P 500 sectors finished higher. The Dow closing above 52,000 confirmed the rotation absorbed the tech selling rather than spreading into a broad selloff. For the week, the Nasdaq remains on track to fall more than 2%, the S&P 500 nearly 1%, while the Dow is poised for a weekly gain of about 1%, per TradingView.

Macro and policy

September producer prices jumped 1.3% month-on-month and 6.3% year-on-year — the largest monthly gain since March 2022 and the largest annual rise since December 2022 — well above consensus of about +0.5% on the month, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics via Reuters. Energy drove the print: producer energy prices surged 10.8%, with gasoline up 20.3%, and diesel and jet fuel also sharply higher, a pass-through from elevated oil prices linked to the US-Israeli war with Iran. Core PPI excluding food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% on the month and 5.0% on the year, so the pressure was not energy-only.

Retail sales rose 0.8% in September, beating the roughly 0.4% consensus, but the underlying trend slowed: sales excluding autos and gasoline rose only about 0.2%, and the control group that feeds GDP rose 0.1%, after strong August gains, per Reuters and Continuum Economics. Gasoline stations accounted for much of the headline strength, with gasoline sales up 6.4% on price, while eating and drinking places rose 1.1%. Weekly initial jobless claims remained low, and the Philadelphia Fed and Empire State surveys were both consistent with a firm economy.

The policy debate tightened. Traders put the odds of a rate hike at the Fed's October 27–28 meeting at about 34%, up from less than 18% a week earlier, per CME FedWatch via the Seoul Economic Daily, though financial markets still leaned toward December as the more likely timing, per Reuters. Oxford Economics argued the Fed could move this month on stickier inflation and a solid labor market. Paradoxically, bonds rallied hard on the day — investors appeared more worried about stress in AI financing than about inflation itself, and bought Treasuries into the uncertainty.

Sector performance

Eight sectors rose and three fell, per Reuters and closing sector snapshots:

  • Health Care led, up 1.84% — the sector hit all-time highs, with UnitedHealth and other large health names drawing the defensive bid.
  • Materials rose 1.71%, as metals rebounded with gold and silver.
  • Financials rose 1.38%, helped by the yield curve and solid bank earnings season backdrop.
  • Consumer Staples rose — the sector is up more than 8% so far in October on the defensive rotation, and retailers contributed on firm retail sales.
  • Consumer Discretionary, Real Estate, Communication Services and Industrials also closed higher. Nike drove discretionary, Salesforce and Home Depot led Dow consumer and industrial strength, and software names flashed buy signals as chips lagged.
  • Energy fell 1.55%, despite WTI crude settling higher — refining and integrated names lagged the commodity.
  • Information Technology fell 0.54% — a much smaller loss than the morning's roughly 1.8% drop, but still the cap on the S&P 500, with AI hardware and servers leading declines.
  • Utilities fell 0.21%, the smallest decline, as falling yields limited the damage to rate-sensitive defensives.

Top movers

  • Nike (NKE) rose 5.21% to $37.14, leading the Dow, per Finnhub closing data. Salesforce rose about 4.5% and Home Depot about 4.3%, per InvestingLive via ad-hoc news, as consumer and software names caught the rotation bid. Walmart and Merck each advanced about 2%.
  • CoreWeave (CRWV) reversed to close up 5.94% at $69.69, after falling as much as 9.5% intraday, after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said at a Bloomberg event that CoreWeave's strength is improving every day, per WSJ and Finnhub. Its bonds also recovered on the day, per Reuters.
  • AI servers were the losers: Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) fell 7.10% to $58.63 and Dell (DELL) fell 6.80% to $473.39, per Finnhub. Vertiv also fell about 6% intraday after analyst price-target cuts. Micron (MU) was the chip exception, rebounding 1.87%, while Seagate fell 3.19% and Western Digital fell 2.10%.
  • Magnificent Seven were mixed: Microsoft rose 1.08%, Alphabet rose 0.45% and Amazon rose 0.27%, while Nvidia fell 1.84%, Tesla fell 1.29%, Apple fell 0.28% and Meta fell 0.11%, per closing snapshots.
  • TSMC (TSM) reported a record quarter and still could not lift chips: third-quarter net profit jumped 69% year-on-year to T$763.1 billion (about $23.6 billion), beating the LSEG SmartEstimate, on US$46.72 billion of revenue, per Reuters — but management struck a cautious note on 2027, and US chip shares stayed under pressure.
  • Anthropic's numbers landed with a thud in the AI valuation debate: the company told investors third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion, up about 45–46% sequentially, per WSJ and Reuters — strong growth, but not enough to silence questions about a possible $2 trillion IPO valuation, with some investors arguing for closer to $1.5 trillion, per IBD/Reuters.

Technicals and sentiment

The VIX fell 2.43% to 16.46, per closing snapshots, after hitting a two-week high intraday — fear spiked in the morning and faded into the close, consistent with rotation rather than capitulation.

Treasury yields fell sharply despite the hot PPI. The 10-year yield dropped about 10 basis points to 5.086% at the close, per eOption's macro table and Reuters, after trading above 5.32% in Wednesday's post-CPI spike. The 2-year yield fell to about 4.75%, and the curve flattened. The dollar index slid about 0.5% to about 102.03 — its biggest fall in six weeks, per Reuters — with the euro up to about $1.1211 and the Swiss franc the day's biggest gainer.

Commodities split. WTI crude settled up $2.88, or 3.03%, at $97.97 a barrel after the EIA counted an 8.6-million-barrel draw in US crude stocks, while Brent settled down 0.13% at $105.92, per eOption and commodities wraps. December gold settled up $62.20, or 1.51%, at $4,170.10 an ounce as the dollar and yields slipped, and silver rose about 3%. Bitcoin traded around $82,300 in early Friday Asia hours after briefly dipping below $80,000.

Key levels into Friday: the S&P 500 reclaimed and held the 7,686–7,725 zone that marked Wednesday's close area; a hold above it keeps the rotation thesis intact. The Nasdaq needs to hold 26,300 to avoid a deeper drawdown from its 27,599.89 record, per TradingNews' levels. The 10-year at about 5.09% remains the swing variable.

Asia-Pacific markets

Asia was the epicenter of the global AI selloff on Thursday. South Korea's KOSPI plunged 7.57% to 5,554.22 — a six-month low and its seventh straight decline, with an intraday drop of more than 9% triggering a circuit breaker — as Samsung Electronics fell 9.5% and SK Hynix fell about 11%, per SKN and AP. Japan's Nikkei 225 tumbled 4.90% to 64,425.95, with SoftBank down about 9.4%, Tokyo Electron down about 7.3% and Kioxia down more than 11%. Taiwan's Taiex fell about 3%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.03% to 24,062.72, mainland China's Shanghai Composite fell 1.59% to 3,707.48, and Australia's ASX 200 edged down 0.1% to about 8,715.60. India was the outlier: the Sensex rose 1.76% to 74,063.85.

The read-through matters for Friday: Asia sold off on Wednesday's US CPI shock and the AI credit stress, before the US cash market reversed. Whether Seoul stabilizes on Friday will be the first signal on whether the deleveraging is done.

US futures overnight

Overnight futures were mixed and modest in early evening trading: Dow futures rose about 0.1%, S&P 500 futures edged higher, and Nasdaq-100 futures fell about 0.1%, per IBD's latest overnight update — a snapshot, not a close. The overnight debate centered on Anthropic's valuation, after Reuters reported some investors favor a $1.5 trillion IPO target rather than $2 trillion, and on Tesla's next-generation Roadster event on Thursday night. Crude futures eased slightly overnight. Overnight action does not necessarily translate into the next regular session.

Next-day outlook and economic calendar

Friday, October 16: September import and export prices at 8:30 AM ET, and September industrial production later in the morning, per the economic calendar cited by ad-hoc news and TradingNews. The Fed's pre-meeting blackout begins Saturday ahead of the October 27–28 meeting.

Earnings before the open: Travelers (TRV) — consensus about $7.25 per share on about $11.31 billion of revenue, per MarketBeat — and Truist Financial (TFC) — consensus about $1.14 per share on about $5.35 billion of revenue, with its call at 8:00 AM ET, per Zolmax/MarketBeat. Both are reads on the financial sector that led Thursday's rotation.

The question for Friday is whether the rotation holds without the bond rally. Thursday's rebound needed a 10-basis-point drop in the 10-year yield; if yields back up on import prices or industrial production, the AI complex — already on a seven-session chip losing streak — is the first place pressure returns. If Seoul stabilizes and Travelers and Truist deliver, the Dow-led broadening can continue into the weekend.
