🔥 Utilities +2.4% — the market's most rate-sensitive sector is leading the S&P 500 as Treasury yields pull back from 24-year highs. Data as of 11:40 AM ET.

Why it's moving

  • 📉 Bond relief: The 10-year Treasury yield slipped to ~5.26% this morning, down from yesterday's 5.31% close — after touching a 24-year high above 5.34% overnight (per Investopedia and Reuters).
  • 🏦 Fed repricing: Odds of an October Fed rate hike collapsed to 23% from 71% a week ago, after top policymakers stressed the need for more data before tightening again (per Reuters).
  • 💼 Soft jobs data: Last week's weak employment figures continue to fuel the dovish repricing that has lifted equities to start the week.
  • 📊 Confirmed leadership: Investor's Business Daily's live coverage has "utilities and industrials the leading sectors" in today's session.

Driving it

The regulated electric utilities are doing the heavy lifting: Southern (SO) +1.2% at $84.83, American Electric Power (AEP) +1.0% at $120.94, and Duke Energy (DUK) +0.7% at $114.64 (per Finnhub, intraday).

Technicals

  • SO +1.2% — still below the 20-day MA ($85.38) and 50-day MA ($89.33); support $81.69 (52-week low), resistance $86.15; basing near lows for five sessions, grinding higher intraday.
  • AEP +1.0% — reclaiming the 20-day MA ($120.83) but under the 50-day MA ($123.85); support $116.71, resistance $121.80; five straight choppy sessions, breaking higher today.
  • DUK +0.7% — below the 20-day MA ($116.73) and 50-day MA ($120.87); support $112.40 (52-week low), resistance $118.00; sideways near lows, bounce still early-stage.

Takeaway

Utilities are bouncing from deeply oversold levels — the yield pullback is relief, not a trend change; the group needs to reclaim its 50-day moving averages before this rally means anything.
