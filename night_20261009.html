US Evening Report · Oct 9

Friday, October 9, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Market close

US stocks rebounded Friday, as tech shares bounced back from Thursday's OpenAI-revenue scare and President Trump's pledge of no Iran strike before the midterm elections eased oil prices. The S&P 500 gained 0.59% (46.18 points) to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.64% (172.83 points) to 27,366.17, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.83% (423.31 points) to 51,654.95, per HK Businesswire, Reuters and Investopedia. The Russell 2000 rose 0.46% to 2,806.98.

The weekly scorecard was positive across the board: the S&P 500 gained 1.2%, the Dow 0.9%, and the Nasdaq 0.6% — the Nasdaq's fourth consecutive weekly gain, per Investopedia and the WSJ. The S&P 500 remains within reach of Tuesday's all-time high and is up more than 13% year-to-date, with the AI-driven bull market approaching its four-year anniversary, per Reuters.

Macro and policy

Consumer mood hit a fresh post-pandemic low. The University of Michigan's preliminary October consumer sentiment index fell to 46.3 from 48.1 in September, missing the 47.6 consensus; the current-conditions gauge crashed to a record low of 44.7 from 50.9, while the expectations index edged up to 47.3 from 46.3, per the WSJ, CNN and Econoday. Year-ahead inflation expectations ticked up to 4.7% from 4.6%, and the five-year reading rose to 3.5% — the highest since May, per the WSJ.

Bond pressure eased into the close. The 10-year Treasury yield ended the week at 5.25%, down about 4 basis points, with the 30-year at 5.60% and the 2-year at 4.80%, per Stocktwits' weekly wrap. Rate futures put the odds of a December hike near 85%, per TradingNews, and Fed minutes confirmed all 19 officials backed the September increase.

Oil settled higher despite Trump's comments. WTI settled up 0.4% at $91.85 a barrel (+0.8% on the week) and Brent gained 0.4% to $104.72 (+2.4% on the week), per the WSJ, after Trump said the US would not strike Iran before the November 3 midterms and described talks with Tehran as "productive." Supply stayed tight: Hurricane Isaias shut in 1.28 million barrels a day (62.9%) of Gulf of Mexico production as a Category 3 storm approaching landfall late Friday, per TradingNews and Reuters. Trump also announced a Russian agreement to supply diesel through domestic and international channels, per Stocktwits.

Sector performance

Tech led the rebound, with software and the Magnificent Seven (the MAGS ETF up about 1%) recovering Thursday's losses, per Investopedia. Communication Services was the session's clear outlier, dragged down by the telecom collapse on the SpaceX spectrum news. Energy was roughly flat, as crude gave back early losses to settle modestly higher. Defensives held firm as yields eased into the close, and financials ticked up ahead of next week's big-bank earnings.

Top movers

  • Telecoms collapsed on news that Elon Musk's SpaceX agreed to acquire a nationwide low-band spectrum portfolio, clearing a path for Starlink Mobile to become a major US carrier: T-Mobile US (TMUS) plunged 13.3% to $148.58, AT&T (T) sank 9.8% to $22.17 and Verizon (VZ) slid 8.8% to $41.65, the three biggest S&P 500 decliners, per Investopedia, Barron's and the Motley Fool. Verizon traded 88.6 million shares, about 286% above its three-month average.
  • Tower operators soared on the flip side: Crown Castle (CCI) jumped 15.6% and American Tower (AMT) gained 9.3%, per Barron's.
  • Oracle (ORCL) rebounded 4.2%, recovering most of Thursday's 5.5% slide as AI-revenue fears faded, per Barron's.
  • Humana (HUM) soared 11% after announcing improved Medicare Advantage plan star ratings for 2027, per Investopedia.
  • Delta Air Lines (DAL) fell about 2.5% after cutting its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $5.10–$5.60 from $6.50–$7.50, as fuel costs — up 62% year-on-year to $4.1 billion in Q3 — added roughly $6 billion to its 2026 bill, per Reuters. Q3 adjusted EPS of $1.72 narrowly missed the $1.76 estimate.

Technicals and sentiment

The VIX hovered around the mid-15s, roughly flat on the day — still calm by historical standards, consistent with orderly dip-buying rather than fear.

The 10-year Treasury yield ended the week at 5.25%, down about 4 basis points; the 30-year sat near 5.60% and the 2-year at 4.80%, per Stocktwits — well off Wednesday's 24-year intraday high of 5.368% on the 10-year.

Gold futures rose about 1.6% to roughly $4,199 an ounce, closing near $4,194.40, as a softer dollar and easing yields boosted bullion, per Reuters and Armstrong Economics. Silver jumped about 3% to near $61. Bitcoin bounced about 1.1% to roughly $82,600, recovering from Thursday's $80,337 low, after Trump's no-strike comments steadied risk appetite, per CoinDesk and ConvExTrade — though US spot ETFs shed $729 million across Wednesday and Thursday, per CryptoCompass.

Asia-Pacific overnight

Asia closed mixed Friday in holiday-thinned trading, with South Korea and Taiwan shut. Japan's Nikkei 225 was about flat at 69,030.92, Shanghai's composite edged up 0.05% to 3,813.79, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng surged 1.79% to 24,211.35 on heavy turnover as Chinese tech names bounced, per Armstrong Economics and Sharecast. India's Sensex jumped 1.23% to 72,472.33 and the Nifty 50 added 1.30% to 22,520.45 after TCS's strong quarter, per The Hindu BusinessLine. Australia's ASX 200 rose 0.64%.

Europe finished green: the STOXX 600 rose 0.7%, the DAX added 1.13%, the CAC 40 gained 0.95% and the FTSE 100 rose 1.06%, per MarketBlast and Armstrong Economics.

US futures overnight

US futures pointed to a calm open after the close: S&P e-minis were up about 24 handles overnight, per MarketBlast, with crude and yields steady in Asia trading.

Outlook: the week ahead

Monday, October 12 is Columbus Day: US equities trade normally but the bond market is closed and no major economic data is scheduled, per Morningstar and Econoday. It is a quiet tape day.

The real action starts Tuesday, October 13: JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo kick off big-bank earnings, joined by UnitedHealth and Johnson & Johnson; the NFIB small-business optimism index and September existing home sales (10:00 AM ET) are also due, per Morningstar and Schaeffer's.

Wednesday, October 14 brings September's CPI at 8:30 AM ET — consensus sees headline month-on-month at 0.6% vs 0.4% in August (year-on-year 3.6% vs 3.4%) and core month-on-month at 0.2% vs 0.3% — plus the Fed's Beige Book at 2:00 PM ET, with Bank of America and Morgan Stanley earnings, per Morningstar.

Thursday, October 15: September PPI, retail sales, weekly jobless claims and the Empire State manufacturing survey at 8:30 AM ET; Taiwan Semiconductor and Charles Schwab report; Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack speaks after the close, per Fidelity/Econoday. Friday, October 16: industrial production and import/export prices.
