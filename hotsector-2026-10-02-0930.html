🔥 Semiconductors +2.4% — chips lead the jobs-report rally

Semiconductors +2.4% — chips are the hottest corner of the market this morning, riding a soft September jobs report that cooled rate-hike fears and reignited the AI trade.

💼 Why it's moving

  • 📉 Soft jobs print — Employers added just 29,000 jobs in September vs. ~90,000 expected, and unemployment ticked up to 4.2% from 4.1%, according to the Labor Department via Reuters and AP. Wage growth of 3.0% year over year was the smallest since May 2021, per AP.
  • 📊 Dovish repricing — The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 5.16% from 5.24%, per AP, and prediction markets cut the odds of an October Fed hike to ~18% from 25%, per MarketWatch.
  • 🤖 AI capex keeps coming — Broadcom's Wall Street syndicate is assembling $60 billion in financing to fund AI chips for Anthropic and others, per Bloomberg; Amazon is offloading ~$8 billion of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips via a special-purpose vehicle, per the FT.
  • 💰 M&A bid — onsemi made a $123-per-share cash bid for Synaptics, sending SYNA up +14%, per TNN.

🚀 Driving it

The move spans AI/logic chips, foundry, equipment, and a fresh M&A bid:

  • AMD +3.4% — logic/AI chips
  • INTC +3.7% — foundry/AI momentum
  • NVDA +2.3% — AI accelerators
  • AVGO +1.5% — networking/AI chips
  • SYNA +14.1% — onsemi's cash bid
  • Equipment strong too: LRCX +2.0%, AMAT +2.0%, TSM +1.4%

📈 Technicals

  • NVDA +2.3% — above the 20-day MA ($223.90) and 50-day MA ($218.32); support $208.93, resistance $236.45 (testing it); hourly uptrend, 15-min grinding higher off the open.
  • AMD +3.4% — above the 20-day MA ($566.59) and 50-day MA ($513.58); support $458, resistance $639; hourly uptrend +4.7% over 5 sessions, 15-min flat near highs.
  • INTC +3.7% — above the 20-day MA ($111.88) and 50-day MA ($100.97); support $91.87, resistance $127.44; hourly uptrend +5.9%, 15-min drifting up.
  • AVGO +1.5% — below the 20-day MA ($353.80) and 50-day MA ($373.10); support $335.81, resistance $372.70; hourly recovering, 15-min nudging up.

Takeaway: A too-soft jobs report took the Fed's foot off the gas — and when rates stop rising, chips start running.

Homebuilders (XHB +2.1%) are the runner-up on the same rate relief, while energy (XLE -0.8%) lags as crude cools.
