The day's conclusion on the whole US market — Tuesday, September 29, 2026. All index values are official closing levels (4:00 p.m. ET) unless noted.

Market close: Williams soothes, yields still bite

Wall Street closed lower for a second straight session, but the afternoon looked much better than the morning. Stocks bounced off their lows after New York Fed President John Williams said there was "no need for urgency" on rate hikes ahead of the October meeting — cutting traders' odds of an October hike to about 50% from 71% on Monday. The catch: long-dated yields refused to stand down.

  • S&P 500: 7,670.84, down 12.9 points (−0.2%), holding just above its 50-day moving average
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 51,349.92, down 131.6 points (−0.3%), a fresh three-month low
  • Nasdaq Composite: 26,797.54, down 22.8 points (−0.1%); the Nasdaq 100 managed a 0.2% gain
  • Russell 2000: 2,807, down 0.4%, also a three-month low

Breadth was better than Monday's: a tech rebound that faded at the highs, a Dow that never found a bid, and small caps that swung from red to green early before slipping again. Soft JOLTS and consumer-confidence prints added to the caution.

Macro & policy: the 10-year's multi-decade squeeze continues

Bonds remained the market's gravity well. The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.26%, its highest closing level since May 2002, after touching 5.293% intraday — a fresh post-2007 high. The 30-year yield closed at 5.59%, its highest since June 2002, after briefly topping 5.62%. The 2-year fell back to about 4.89%, which is where Williams' "no urgency" comment did its work. Futures now price roughly 50% odds of an October quarter-point hike, down from 71% Monday, and 43% odds of a half-point of hikes through year-end.

Oil pulled back hard. WTI fell 3.5% to settle at $89.38 a barrel — its lowest in a week — after Saudi Arabia reportedly restored roughly half the capacity of its East-West pipeline and the Trump administration ordered another emergency-reserve release, while US-Iran talks stayed deadlocked in the war's eighth month. Brent fell 2.6% to $102.59. Cheaper oil eased pressure on Asia overnight but couldn't lift US equities against 5.2%-plus yields.

The morning data cut both ways. August JOLTS job openings came in soft at 7.08 million versus 7.23 million expected, and September consumer confidence unexpectedly plunged to its lowest since 2014 — both reasons for the Fed to tread carefully, and both reminders the economy is cooling. In the policy churn: a Reuters exclusive on Anthropic's IPO prospectus showed revenue up 12-fold last year against operating losses above $8 billion and net losses over $40 billion, targeting a valuation north of $2 trillion; President Trump said working with China on AI would hurt US companies while rejecting new AI regulations — read as tech-friendly; and Trump denied an Axios report that he had offered Iran sanctions relief. China unveiled a rate cut plus mortgage subsidies to spur growth, and the dollar rose 0.3% to a two-month high as the Aussie fell 0.7% after an RBA hike.

Sectors: utilities lead a quiet rotation

Sector breadth was muted: 4 of 11 S&P sectors finished higher. Defensive utilities (+1.1%) led, followed by communication services (+0.4%), industrials (+0.2%), and consumer discretionary (+0.1%). The rest slipped: real estate (−0.01%), information technology (−0.3%), health care (−0.3%), financials (−0.4%), consumer staples (−0.5%), materials (−0.6%), and energy (−0.9%), the biggest loser as crude tumbled. The PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) rebounded 1.3% and the memory ETF popped about 3% after Monday's chip rout.

Asia-Pacific: Tuesday's red, Wednesday's rebound

Tuesday's Asian session fell on the same yield shock that hit Wall Street on Monday. Nikkei 225: 65,199.41, down 680 points (−1.0%) with TOPIX −1.7%; Tokyo tech exporters bore the brunt as traders priced in more Bank of Japan tightening. KOSPI defended 6,870 with little change as foreigners sold a net W2.9 trillion, though chipmakers like Samsung Electronics rebounded from Monday's rout. Hang Seng: 24,506.12, down 135 points (−0.6%), weighed down by property developers and platform tech. Shanghai Composite: 3,830.45, up 0.2%, supported by PBoC liquidity injections and state-backed buying. TAIEX: 47,631.96, down 321 points (−0.7%) as TSMC retreated 0.8% from its record close.

Wednesday's early trade looks greener: the MSCI Asia Pacific Index is up 0.4%, the Nikkei is climbing 1.6%, the Topix is up 0.9%, and the KOSPI is up 0.7% — cheaper oil and the US semiconductor rebound easing the mood. But Hang Seng futures slipped 0.3%, pointing to a softer Hong Kong open, and India's GIFT Nifty hinted at a red October-series start. Yen steady at 157.36 per dollar; offshore yuan at 6.7073.

US futures overnight

Dow futures are up about 0.16%, S&P 500 futures up 0.16%, and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.26% on Globex late Tuesday — a tentative steadying after the losing session, with the 10-year yield dipping to 5.23%. Futures quotes as of late Tuesday night ET. The mood is a holding pattern: nobody wants to be aggressively positioned ahead of Wednesday morning's inflation data.

Wednesday & the week ahead

Wednesday is the week's first real test. 8:15 a.m. ET: September ADP employment (forecast 70K, up from 38K). 8:30 a.m. ET: August core PCE — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — expected at 0.3% month-on-month (versus 0.2% prior), with headline PCE and the final Q2 GDP print (1.5%) alongside personal income and spending. 9:45 a.m.: Chicago PMI (forecast 51.3 versus 47.1). Overnight, watch China's September PMIs, Japan's industrial production and retail sales, and Germany's flash CPI.

On earnings, Micron reports after the close — the AI-memory bellwether, with HBM, DRAM and NAND pricing and next-quarter guidance as the focal points for the whole semiconductor complex. Conagra, Jabil, FactSet and Cal-Maine report before the open. Thursday: ISM manufacturing, Fed Governor Waller speaks, and Nike earnings. Friday, October 2: the September jobs report.

The bottom line: Tuesday showed the market can live with 5.26% on the 10-year — for a day. Williams' "no urgency" line took October hike odds off the boil, and cheaper oil plus a chip rebound kept the Nasdaq's losses marginal. But the S&P 500 is still riding its 50-day line and the Dow is at a three-month low. Wednesday's core PCE decides whether the bond selloff pauses or resumes — and everything rate-sensitive will move on that number.
