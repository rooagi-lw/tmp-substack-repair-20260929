The day's conclusion on the whole US market — Monday, September 28, 2026. All index values are official closing levels (4:00 p.m. ET) unless noted.

Market close: yields spoil the week opener

Wall Street started the week with broad losses. A fresh multi-decade surge in Treasury yields — the 10-year touched its highest since 2007 and the 30-year its highest since 2002 — plus an early oil spike after President Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, kept sellers in charge into the close.

  • S&P 500: 7,683.69, down 59.7 points (−0.8%), its lowest in more than a week
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 51,481.51, down 347.1 points (−0.7%), approaching a three-month low
  • Nasdaq Composite: 26,820.38, down 248.3 points (−0.9%), also a one-week-plus low
  • Russell 2000: down 0.7%

The indexes gave back a good portion of Friday's rally (S&P +0.5%, Dow +0.9% on September 25) as the bond market, not earnings, set the tape.

Macro & policy: the bond selloff deepens, the Fed looms

Bonds were the story. The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.241%, a fresh 19-year high — the seventh new 19-year closing high this month — after touching 5.272% intraday, close to a 24-year high. The 30-year yield rose to 5.561%, its highest since 2002. The 2-year yield climbed to 4.922%, a level last seen in May 2024. Futures markets are now pricing a roughly 70% chance of another Fed rate hike at the October meeting — up from 58% a week ago and 18% a month ago — after the Fed raised rates earlier this month, its first increase since 2023. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson both struck hawkish notes, warning against letting elevated prices become normal.

Oil was the day's other shock. Brent surged more than 3% after President Trump rejected Iran's proposal for a seven-day ceasefire tied to reopening the Strait of Hormuz — saying Tehran had overplayed its hand — then pared the gain as Qatari mediators prepared to meet both sides and Saudi Aramco resumed crude loading at the Red Sea port of Yanbu via its repaired East-West pipeline. Brent closed up 0.9% at $105.28 a barrel; WTI settled 19 cents higher at $92.60. Trump said he still expects more talks with Iran this week, even as he left open the possibility of further military action before the November midterms.

A stronger dollar plus rising real yields punished precious metals: gold futures fell $150.80 (−3.5%) to $4,135.40 an ounce, the biggest one-day drop since June and gold's fifth down session in six. Silver lost nearly 5%. The dollar index rose 0.2% to 101.21.

Sectors: defensives hide, everything rate-sensitive sells

Sector breadth was weak: only 3 of 11 S&P sectors finished higher — two defensives (consumer staples and health care) plus energy, none up more than 0.4%. Everything else fell at least 0.4%, with four sectors down more than 1%: communication services, consumer discretionary, financials, and industrials. Super-heavyweight technology lost 0.7% as AI and chip names sold off. The PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 1.6%.

Tuesday & the week ahead

The macro calendar is heavy and every print feeds October hike odds. Tuesday: September consumer confidence (10 a.m. ET) and the JOLTS August job-openings report, plus MongoDB's Investor Day. Wednesday: August PCE inflation (the Fed's preferred gauge). Thursday: ISM manufacturing. Friday, October 2: the September jobs report. Also on the earnings docket: Micron and Nike report this week, and AMD's World Labs deal adds another AI-M&A headline.

The bottom line: Monday's selloff was all about one factor — oil pushing up inflation expectations, expectations pushing up yields, and yields pushing down every rate-sensitive asset. The 10-year above 5.2% is now the market's gravity well. Whether Tuesday's data loosens its grip depends on what consumers tell us about the economy's temperature.
