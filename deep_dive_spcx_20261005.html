SpaceX jumped 7% on Friday and another 5.5% Monday after the busiest launch day in its history. But the stock is still 25% below its June peak, Morgan Stanley calls it "unusually cheap," and one top investor won't touch it above $90. Here is what actually matters.

What happened

  • 🚀 Three launches in under 13 hours on Thursday, October 1 — only the second time SpaceX has pulled that off. A Falcon 9 carried NASA's Crew-13 (four astronauts, the fastest-ever US trip to the ISS at under eight hours), a second Falcon 9 flew 130 payloads including Google's Project Suncatcher orbital-AI test chip, and a Falcon Heavy launched the classified NROL-97 — the first-ever NRO mission on a Falcon Heavy (per SpaceX reporting via Fool.com and Interesting Engineering).
  • 📈 SPCX surged 7% on Friday to $158.96, its best week in nearly two months, then climbed another 5.5% Monday to $167.72 — the highest since July (Finnhub).
  • 🤖 Musk teased a rebrand of the AI unit on Sunday: "No more AI," "SI," "It's better." SpaceXAI could become SpaceXSI, following President Trump's September 29 executive order directing federal agencies to adopt "Super Intelligence" terminology. It would be the unit's third identity since SpaceX absorbed xAI in February (Stocktwits/TradingView).
  • 📊 Morgan Stanley reiterated Overweight with a $300 price target — about 79% above Monday's close — calling shares "unusually cheap" ahead of Starship Flight 15 (Investopedia).

How we got here

June 12, 2026 — the biggest IPO in history. SpaceX priced at $135, sold 555.6 million shares for $75 billion ($85.7B with the greenshoe), roughly 3.3x Saudi Aramco's prior record. It closed day one at $160.95, up 19%, with half a billion shares traded (Financer, Fintechbits).

June 16 — peak euphoria. The stock hit $225.64 in its third session, valuing the company near $2.9 trillion.

August 4 — the earnings beat nobody liked. Q2 revenue of $7.81 billion (+92% Y/Y) beat the ~$6.88B consensus, and the loss of -$0.09/share beat expectations of -$0.26. But the AI segment burned $15.8 billion in capex in a single quarter, free cash flow was -$25 billion, and the stock fell ~7% after hours (Financial-News.co.uk, Stock Moguls). It bottomed at $104.83 — a 54% peak-to-trough drawdown in seven weeks.

September 28 — Starship reaches orbit, with an asterisk. Flight 14 was the first Starship to reach Earth orbit and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, but a Raptor Vacuum engine shut down on ascent and the flight ended early. Morgan Stanley graded it B+ and warned that recurring vacuum-engine failures on Flights 12 and 14 suggest a systemic V3 propulsion problem (Catenaa, TheStreet).

September 22 — the COO sold. Gwynne Shotwell exercised options and sold 342,170 shares at ~$153.57 for $52.5 million under a 10b5-1 plan adopted in June — the first major insider sale since the IPO. She retains about $830 million in stock (Cryptonomist, BeInCrypto).

By the numbers

SpaceX now reports three segments, and only one of them makes money:

  • Connectivity (Starlink): $4.29B revenue (+66%), +$1.66B operating profit. 12 million subscribers (doubled in a year), 167 markets. The catch: ARPU slid to $66/month from $85 as international mix grew. Enterprise and government connectivity revenue more than doubled to $1.8B+. Capex was just $1.4B — this is the business that funds everything else (Financial-News.co.uk, Fool.com).
  • AI (xAI/Grok): $2.56B revenue (+247%), -$1.26B operating loss. The revenue engine: Anthropic pays $1.25 billion per month through May 2029 for 220,000+ Nvidia GPUs; Google pays ~$920M/month. Combined contracted future revenue is roughly $75 billion. In late September Musk said another 220,000 Nvidia GB300 GPUs were going live, with more in November (Cryptonomist, Crypto Briefing, 404k Research).
  • Space (launch): $962M revenue (+29%), -$542M operating loss. Still the prestige business, still not the profit business.

Total: $7.81B revenue (+92%), -$143M operating loss, $100B cash vs $39.7B debt. The company has never posted a GAAP profit; the accumulated deficit was $41.3B at the end of Q1 (Financer).

Why it matters

SpaceX at $2.16 trillion trades at roughly 68x trailing sales while losing money — a multiple higher than Rocket Lab's 53x, even though SpaceX is ~40x Rocket Lab's size and growing faster off a far larger base (Foreign Policy Journal). The bull case isn't about rockets at all: Morgan Stanley's $300 sum-of-the-parts assigns $165 of the $300 to Enterprise AI — the idea that SpaceX becomes a hyperscale AI cloud. Starlink is the proof the company can build a profitable infrastructure business; the question is whether the AI compute buildout, burning $15.8B of capex a quarter, gets there before the cash math gets uncomfortable.

Bull vs bear

The bull case: Adam Jonas at Morgan Stanley argues SPCX is "unusually cheap" on a growth-adjusted basis versus mega-cap AI peers, with investor interest "really, really quiet" — a setup he likes ahead of Flight 15 and the AI capacity ramp. TD Cowen upgraded to strong-buy with a $200 target on September 30, and Cathie Wood's ARK bought ~$37M after the post-earnings dip. The logic: a self-funding Starlink cash engine plus $75B of contracted AI revenue plus a $100B cash fortress.

The bear case: Wall Street Zen cut the stock to Sell on October 3. DZ Bank's $100 target implies ~40% downside. Top-ranked TipRanks investor Oliver Rodzianko cut to Hold, arguing "SpaceX can be dominating, and still too expensive" — he'd only buy at $90, preferably $60. The logic: 68x sales with negative earnings, -$25B quarterly free cash flow, unresolved Starship propulsion issues, and a lockup calendar that keeps unlocking shares (employee tranches October 10 and 25, ~900M more shares after Q3 results, full expiry December 8; Musk's own ~6.4B shares locked until June 2027).

What to watch next

  1. Starship Flight 15 (no earlier than ~October 19, tentative). The potential first catch of a Block 3 booster — the reusability milestone Morgan Stanley's valuation leans on. Date unconfirmed; needs Flight 14 review and FAA sign-off (NASASpaceflight).
  2. Q3 earnings (early November, date unconfirmed). The second report as a public company — watch whether the AI segment's $1.26B operating loss narrows on the new cloud deals.
  3. Lockup tranches October 10 and 25. Each releases roughly 7% more employee shares. The stock absorbed the August 6 tranche; the market will watch the next two.
  4. The SI rebrand and any Grok/Starlink Mobile product news. Musk moves fast on naming; product timelines matter more.

Takeaway

SpaceX is three companies wearing one ticker: a profitable satellite-internet utility, a capital-hungry AI cloud, and a rocket company that loses money on launches. At $2.16 trillion, you are not paying for the rockets or even Starlink — you are paying for the AI compute bet to work. Everything between now and year-end is about whether Flight 15 and the GPU ramp make that bet look safer.
