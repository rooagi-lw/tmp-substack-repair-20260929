US Evening Report · Oct 1

Thursday, October 1, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET.

Market close: a narrow win for the bulls

U.S. stocks began October with a modest, selective advance as a retreat in Treasury yields offset persistent inflation and energy worries. The S&P 500 rose 14.91 points (0.19%) to close at 7,666.45, the Nasdaq Composite edged up 10.53 points (0.04%) to 26,871.60, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 20.51 points (0.04%) to finish at 50,926.56, according to preliminary closing data. The Russell 2000 small-cap index gained 9.76 points (0.35%) to 2,806.63.

The session reversed course in the afternoon: indexes had opened lower as the 10-year yield spiked to a 24-year high, then flipped green as yields pulled back. All three majors remain on pace for weekly losses, with the Dow off about 1.7% on the week.

Macro & policy: the yield is the market

The dominant story is bonds. The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.344% in the morning — its highest level since 2002 — before settling at 5.24%, down about 5 basis points on the day, per CNN and Treasury market data. The 30-year yield hit a 24-year high near 5.69% before easing to 5.61%. The bond market just completed its worst quarter this century, with the 10-year yield up roughly 87 basis points over the September quarter.

The yield spike eased after a cooler-than-expected August inflation report. Core personal consumption expenditures rose 0.2% on the month versus 0.3% expected, and the annual rate printed 3.0% versus 3.3% — most of the difference coming from downward revisions to June and July under a new calculation method. Markets are now pricing in only a 28.2% chance of a Fed rate hike of at least 25 basis points at the October meeting, down from 68.6% a week earlier, according to CME FedWatch — implying roughly a 72% chance the central bank stands pat.

Fed officials struck a split tone. Fed Governor Philip Jefferson suggested the central bank may need more time before adjusting policy. Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari told Bloomberg Television he expects additional rate increases will be needed to restrain the economy going into 2027, though he is unsure whether the next move should happen this month. Separately, New York Fed President John Williams said earlier this week there is no urgency to raise rates, signaling a possible wait until December.

On the data front, September ISM manufacturing showed slower growth than expected alongside rising prices, while jobless claims fell for a fourth straight week, pointing to a stable labor market.

Sector performance: energy leads, health care lags

Five of the eleven S&P 500 sectors closed higher. Energy was the clear leader at +1.92%, followed by industrials (+1.00%) and information technology (+0.76%), utilities (+0.61%) and financials (+0.16%).

On the downside, health care fell 1.30%, communication services lost 1.19%, real estate slipped 0.62%, materials dropped 0.45%, consumer staples eased 0.29%, and consumer discretionary declined 0.15%.

Top movers

  • Accenture (ACN) surged roughly 16% to its highest level since March after fiscal Q4 revenue of $18.7 billion and adjusted EPS of $3.29 both beat consensus, with quarterly bookings of $22.2 billion setting an all-time record — a direct answer to fears that AI is eating consulting demand.
  • Micron (MU) rose about 3% after a blockbuster quarter: revenue quadrupled, and the company issued a roughly $61.5 billion revenue guide — about 13% above expectations — backed by $32 billion in customer commitments, reinforcing faith in the AI trade.
  • Synopsys climbed about 10%, joining the chip rally; the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) and iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) each added roughly 1.5% on gains in Nvidia and Micron.
  • Advancing issues outnumbered decliners by 1.23-to-1 on the NYSE and 1.04-to-1 on the Nasdaq. The S&P 500 posted 4 new 52-week highs against 41 new lows; the Nasdaq recorded 41 new highs and 287 new lows.
  • Nike reported earnings after the closing bell; Alphabet shares were higher after Google announced a new AI model.

Technicals & sentiment

The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) closed at 16.39, up 0.31% — a two-week high reading, though still a moderate level. The bond-market MOVE index gained 3.61% to 110.46, its third straight record high, underscoring that rate volatility, not equity volatility, is the market's real stress point. VIX futures sit in contango, with the front contract at 17.73.

Commodities

WTI crude settled up 2.7% at $92.91 a barrel, while Brent crude gained more than 4% to close at $102.31, retaking $100 after China suspended fuel exports, tightening an already stressed market. The Wall Street Journal reported the U.S. is sending a third aircraft carrier strike group to the Middle East, adding a geopolitical premium. Gold rose 0.5% to $4,207.80 an ounce on a steady haven bid. Bitcoin traded near $84,600, up about 1%.

Asia-Pacific: mixed Friday morning

Asian markets are showing a mixed picture on Friday morning (October 2). Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.88% to 68,380.20, while Australia's S&P/ASX 200 rose 0.29% to 8,639.80 and South Korea's KOSPI gained 0.25% to 6,986.13. Hong Kong's Hang Seng recorded a 0.37% gain. Mainland China's Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges are closed for the National Day/Golden Week holiday, and India's exchanges observe the Gandhi Jayanti holiday.

US futures overnight

Stock futures are little changed Thursday night as traders await Friday's jobs report: S&P 500 futures up about 0.03%, Dow futures up 10 points (0.02%), and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.07%.

Next-day outlook & economic calendar

Friday's September nonfarm payrolls report at 8:30 AM ET is the week's biggest catalyst. The consensus calls for job growth of 84,000 with the unemployment rate holding steady at 4.1%. Given the stable labor market, a strong report alone is unlikely to push the Fed to hike in October, economists say — but any big surprise in either direction could move yields sharply, and yields have been moving stocks all week.
