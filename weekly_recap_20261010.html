The S&P 500 gained 1.2% to its second-highest close in history — just 0.09% below Tuesday's record 7,818.93, per Morningstar's Dow Jones Newswires data. But the market that did the lifting wasn't the one investors have been watching. Nine of eleven sectors rose while technology fell, the equal-weight index outperformed the cap-weighted one, and defensive corners led for a change. The AI trade wobbled midweek on an OpenAI revenue scare, the 10-year touched its highest level since 2002, and Brent crude held above $100. Wednesday's CPI is now the only thing that matters.

Where the week was won and lost

For the first time in months, the week's winners were not the usual suspects. Per Seeking Alpha's weekly tally, consumer staples (+4.0%) led all sectors, followed by utilities (+3.9%) and energy (+3.6%) — with healthcare (+2.8%), financials (+2.3%) and consumer discretionary (+3.1%) also strong. Nine of the eleven sectors finished green. The two that didn't were technology (-0.3%) and industrials (-0.4%), per TradingView/Seeking Alpha's sector recap — tech's lag is the story, given it has carried the index all year.

The breadth healing is the week's most important signal. The equal-weight S&P 500 outperformed the cap-weighted index this week, per Lance Roberts' Bull Bear Report, and only three of eleven sector ETFs still sit below their 50-day moving averages — down from a much worse picture in September. But the AI complex is the outlier: semiconductors fell 4.5% on the week, per the same report, even as the S&P rose. As Roberts put it, a record standing on a few names is not the same as a broad advance — this week, for once, the advance was broad and the few names lagged.

The scoreboard

Friday was a genuine rally: the S&P 500 rose 0.6% to 7,811.54, the Dow added 0.8% to 51,654.95 (about 425 points), the Nasdaq gained 0.6% to 27,366, and the Russell 2000 rose 0.5%, per Investopedia and TradingView. Friday's tape had its own drama — SpaceX's purchase of Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum (about $8 billion, per the WSJ) to build Starlink Mobile into a major US carrier crushed the telecoms while lifting the towers: T-Mobile fell 13.3% in its worst session of the S&P 500's day, Verizon had its worst day since 2002, while Crown Castle surged 15.6%, per the WSJ and CNBC. Humana soared 11% on improved Medicare Advantage star ratings for 2027, and the Magnificent Seven rebounded from Thursday's selloff.

The week, unlike most weeks this year, didn't need Friday to look good. The S&P 500 gained 1.2%, the Dow 0.9%, and the Nasdaq 0.6% — its fourth straight weekly gain, per Investopedia — while the Russell 2000 slipped about 1%, its fifth straight weekly loss, per Barron's. The records came mid-week: the S&P 500 set a record close of 7,818.93 on Tuesday, and the Nasdaq set a record 27,599.79 the same day (back-to-back records after Monday's, per Morningstar and the WSJ) — Friday's closes sit just below both. The Dow set no record; it remains nearly 5% below its August high, per Morningstar. For the year: S&P 500 +14.1%, Nasdaq +17.7%, Dow +7.5%, Russell 2000 about +12.9%, per Morningstar's Data Talk.

And the breadth underneath: the equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) gained 1.6% on the week, outperforming the cap-weighted index's 1.15%, and 65% of the 870 US-listed stocks Morningstar tracks rose on the week.

The bond revolt

Bonds calmed down on the surface and stayed alarming underneath. The 10-year Treasury yield finished Friday at 5.243%, down about 3 basis points on the week — snapping a five-week streak of rising yields, per Morningstar's Dow Jones Newswires Data Talk. But the path there was violent: the 10-year traded at 5.32–5.35% mid-week, its highest since 2002, per Benzinga/MarketWatch and TradingEconomics, and the 30-year hit 5.70%, a fresh 24-year high, per hdfcsky. The 2-year ended at 4.80%, down 3 basis points on the week from 4.83%, per Morningstar's Weekly Market Update.

The driver, again, is the long end pricing inflation risk, not growth: oil near multi-month highs, ISM prices paid at four-year highs, and Wednesday's FOMC minutes showing most officials expect another rate increase by year-end. TLT managed a 0.7% weekly gain as the selling paused, per Stocktwits' weekly bond recap — a breather, not a reversal. With the 10-year still up 1.09 percentage points year to date, per Morningstar, the bond market has not surrendered its veto over this rally.

The data that moved the Fed

A three-act week for rate expectations, again — but this one tilted hawkish. Act one, Monday: September ISM Services came in at 54.9, a touch below the 55.0–55.1 consensus and down from 55.4, per IndexBox and Verified Investing — the 27th straight month of expansion. The headline was fine; the split was not: the Prices index jumped to 74.0, the highest since July 2022 and above the ~72.9 consensus, while Employment crept back above 50 for the first time in three months. A Fed that hiked to 3.75–4.00% in September will not find comfort in a 74.0 prices print.

Act two, Wednesday: minutes from the September FOMC meeting showed most participants expect another increase in the federal funds rate by year-end, per TradingEconomics and Reuters — and all supported September's 25bp hike, the first since 2023, in a unanimous 12–0 vote. Governor Waller said Thursday that additional hikes will likely be needed to bring inflation back to target, while emphasizing "flexibility" around the pace — the hikes, he said, do not need to come at consecutive meetings.

Act three, the labor and consumer reads: weekly jobless claims fell to 197,000, below the 200,000 consensus and the fifth straight decline, with the four-week average at its lowest since October 2022 — a "low-hire, low-fire" market that argues for neither urgency nor panic, per Reuters/Dow Jones Newswires. Friday's preliminary October Michigan sentiment slipped to 46.3, below consensus and down from 48.1, with current conditions plunging to a record-low 44.7 — while year-ahead inflation expectations crept up to 4.7%, the number the Fed actually watches, per Morningstar/Dow Jones Newswires.

Net: markets price roughly an 82% chance the Fed holds at the October 27–28 meeting (confirmed on the Fed's official calendar; Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference follows the statement), with only about 18% odds of a hike — but December is where the action is: roughly 80% odds that rates end the year at 4.00–4.25% or higher, per CME-data trackers cited by multiple outlets, matching the September dot plot's 4.125% median. Wednesday's CPI is the last big input before the meeting.

Energy and gold

Crude stayed hostage to the Middle East. WTI settled Friday at $91.60, up about 0.4% on the week, per Morningstar's Weekly Market Update, while Brent settled at $104.72, up 2.4% — the international benchmark is pricing the shipping risk, with a ~$13 spread over WTI, per Morningstar's Dow Jones Newswires Data Talk. The week's swing: crude jumped about 3% Thursday after The Atlantic reported the White House had asked the Pentagon to prepare Iran strike options that could be carried out before the midterms, alongside attacks on Gulf shipping and a Gulf of Mexico hurricane shutting in roughly 511,000 barrels a day — then retreated Friday after President Trump said Washington was in productive discussions with Iran and would not attack before the November 3 midterms, per hdfcsky. Delta's Friday report showed the cost side: its quarterly fuel bill hit $4.1 billion, up 62% from a year ago.

Gold caught a bid as yields paused: Comex front-month futures gained about 1.2% to $4,191.40, snapping a two-week losing streak after hitting a two-month low mid-week, per Morningstar's Weekly Market Update; spot briefly topped $4,200. The demand backdrop keeps building: China's central bank raised its gold reserves for the 23rd straight month in September.

Earnings scorecard: this week's winners and losers

Winner: Helen of Troy (HELE). The week's cleanest earnings reaction. Thursday's report: adjusted EPS of $0.79 crushed the ~$0.50–0.51 consensus — a beat of roughly 55% — on revenue of $440.9 million, a slight miss of the ~$442 million expected, per MT Newswires. The stock spiked 25% in early trading and closed up about 4–5% at $26.64. Sweetener: full-year adjusted EPS guidance raised to $3.60–$4.15 from $3.25–$3.75. The tempering note, per the Fool: about $0.12 a share of the beat came from roughly $27 million in tariff refunds — the quality is debatable.

Loser: Delta (DAL). The season's first big warning. Q3 adjusted EPS of $1.72 missed the $1.75–1.77 FactSet consensus — Delta's first earnings miss in two years, per Barron's — on adjusted revenue of $17.59 billion versus ~$17.65 billion expected. Fuel did all of it: the quarterly bill hit $4.1 billion, up 62% year over year and $500 million above July guidance, and the full-year fuel cost is now $6 billion above plan. The guidance cut was severe: full-year adjusted EPS of $5.10–$5.60, down from $6.50–$7.50 — a 24% cut at the midpoints — and free cash flow guidance fell to $2.5 billion. The stock fell 3.5% premarket and closed down 1.4% at $81.03, its worst week since late August, per Barron's and Dow Jones Market Data. Demand, notably, is not the problem: Q4 revenue is guided up ~20%.

The beat the market sold: Constellation Brands (STZ). Reported Tuesday after the close: revenue of $2.63 billion beat the ~$2.54 billion expected and comparable EPS of $3.74 beat ~$3.55, per Zacks and Seeking Alpha's transcript summary. The stock fell more than 4% in after-hours trading, then recovered to close Wednesday up 2.1% at $118.09, per ad-hoc-news. The spoilers: beer depletions (the true demand read) fell 0.6% while shipments rose 5.5% on distributor restocking, per the Motley Fool, and the company merely reaffirmed full-year guidance of $11.20–$11.90 — analyst target cuts followed from Needham, Piper Sandler, Morgan Stanley and UBS. Sold off on guidance, then forgiven.

The beat the market really sold: Levi's (LEVI). EPS of $0.48 crushed the $0.36 consensus — but revenue of $1.61 billion came in a touch light of $1.62 billion expected, and the stock closed down about 2% on Wednesday, per StockTwits. The quality question: roughly $0.11 of the beat came from about $80 million in tariff refunds, and FY 2026 revenue guidance was cut to ~$6.7 billion versus ~$6.8 billion consensus — even though FY EPS guidance was raised to $1.54–$1.56 from $1.46–$1.52. Beat-and-raise on paper, tariff-flavored underneath.

The beat the market bought: PepsiCo (PEP). Thursday's print: revenue of $25.27 billion beat ~$24.9 billion and core EPS of $2.34 beat ~$2.30, per Zacks and the WSJ — and the stock rose about 2–3% despite a cut to full-year core EPS growth guidance (2.5–3.5% from 5–7%). International strength (organic revenue +3.1%) outweighed the guide cut; Citi had warned the cut could trigger a negative reaction, and the market disagreed.

The week's biggest reaction wasn't earnings at all: PTC (+35% on the week, the S&P 500's best). France's Schneider Electric agreed Monday to acquire the industrial-software maker for $205 a share in cash, a 42% premium valuing the equity at $22.6 billion, per the WSJ and Barron's — on track for PTC's largest daily percentage gain on record, per Dow Jones Market Data. The read-through the market took: industrial software and AI-adjacent assets still command scarcity premiums.

Next week: the CPI referendum

Wednesday, October 14, 8:30 AM ET: September CPI — the single most important data point of the month, per FXIFY's trading desk brief, and the last major inflation read before the October 27–28 FOMC meeting (WSJ's CPI calendar; NordFX). The WSJ economist survey expects headline +0.6% month over month (+3.7% year over year) and core +0.2% month over month (+2.5% year over year) — a hot print would cement the December hike; a soft one would reopen the pause debate. Thursday is the second-most market-moving day: September PPI at 8:30 AM ET (consensus +0.6% m/m), September retail sales at 8:30 AM ET (consensus +0.4%), plus weekly jobless claims and the Empire State and Philly Fed surveys. Friday brings import prices and industrial production — second-tier, confirming rather than moving. Monday is Columbus Day: equities open, bond markets closed per SIFMA, no federal data, and thin liquidity with Japan and Canada also closed. Then the tape belongs to the banks and the inflation data — in that order.

The takeaway: this was the week the market finally broadened — defensives led, tech lagged, the equal-weight index beat the cap-weighted one, and two indexes set records anyway. That's the healthy version of a rally. But it was built under a 5.24% 10-year and $100+ Brent, and roughly 80% odds say rates end the year at 4.00–4.25% or higher. Breadth can heal in a week; the bond market's veto takes a CPI to lift. Until Wednesday, the trade is simple: watch the long end, not Friday's green close.
