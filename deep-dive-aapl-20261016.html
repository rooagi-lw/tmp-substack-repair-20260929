Apple is the first $5 trillion company, and it is priced like nothing can go wrong. At $342 — 1.8% below the all-time high set this week — the stock trades near 39 times trailing earnings, and the average analyst price target, $340.39 (MarketBeat), sits below where the shares already trade. The next test is close: fiscal fourth-quarter results on Monday, November 2, where Wall Street's consensus — $1.99 a share on about $113.4 billion of revenue — already sits at the very top of the range Apple guided to in July. This is a deep dive on what has to go right, what is already wobbling, and what decides the stock into year-end.

Two weeks that framed the debate

  • The order-cut scare. Reuters reported on October 9, citing Nikkei Asia, that Apple told some suppliers to cut October component production for the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max by at least 15%, on softer demand after a $100 price increase driven by memory-chip costs. The stock fell about 1%, then rebounded on October 12 after JPMorgan's Samik Chatterjee raised his target from $340 to $360, arguing the cuts reflect Apple's new split launch cadence rather than weak demand, and Bank of America (Buy, $370) pointed to the same cadence while noting shorter Pro delivery times. The order cut itself is single-source supply-chain reporting; Apple has not commented, and it remains unconfirmed.
  • The foldable arrives. Pre-orders for the iPhone Duo — Apple's first foldable, priced from $1,999 to $3,199 — opened this morning, October 16, in more than 70 countries including China (MacRumors). It goes on sale October 23, with reported limited supplies.
  • The AI home. At its October 13 "Welcome Home" event, Apple launched HomeView, a $299 smart-home hub built around the new Gemini-based Siri AI, alongside a HomePod mini 2 and a new Apple TV 4K (USA Today). Every one of these is another endpoint for Siri AI across an installed base of more than 2.5 billion devices.
  • China AI, reported but not confirmed. Bloomberg reported on October 14 that Apple Intelligence will launch in mainland China in December with iOS 27.3, using Alibaba and Baidu technology after permits were received in late September. Apple has not confirmed the launch, and the China version reportedly excludes the new Siri AI.
  • A new CEO. John Ternus, formerly the hardware chief, has been CEO since September 1, with Tim Cook as executive chairman (MacRumors). The November 2 call is his first earnings call in the role.
  • Berkshire is standing pat. No stake change has been reported this month; Berkshire's latest filing-based count is about 228 million shares, roughly $66 billion and still its largest holding (American Banking News).

The last quarter, by the numbers

Fiscal Q3, reported July 30 (Apple via Business Wire), was a record June quarter: revenue of $109.4 billion, up 16%, and diluted EPS of $2.02, up 29% and well ahead of the $1.89 consensus. Net income was $29.8 billion, and gross margin reached 50.1% — flattered by roughly two points of tariff refunds, worth about $0.11 of the EPS.

By segment:

  • iPhone: $54.3 billion, +22% — a June-quarter record
  • Services: $30.7 billion, +12% — a record, but below the $31.2 billion consensus
  • Mac: $10.4 billion, +29% — a June-quarter record
  • iPad: $6.2 billion, −6% — the only declining category
  • Wearables, Home & Accessories: $7.9 billion, +6%

Greater China revenue rose 22% to $18.8 billion — strong growth that still missed a roughly $19.5 billion estimate. Services carries a gross margin near 76% and more than 1.5 billion paid subscriptions, which is why a $0.5 billion segment miss matters more than the headline beat.

The tell was the market's reaction: despite the beat, the stock fell about 6–7% after the print, on fiscal Q4 guidance of 9–11% revenue growth and gross margin guided down to 47–48% as the tariff refunds drop out and memory costs bite. Execution was not the problem in July. The bar was.

The iPhone cycle: winning share in a shrinking market

The unit data and the supply-chain data currently tell two different stories, and both are sourced.

The demand data is genuinely strong. IDC's preliminary calendar-Q3 figures (October 14) show global smartphone shipments falling 22% — the steepest quarterly decline on record, driven by memory costs up roughly 350% year over year — while Apple shipped 57.3 million units, down just 4%, and lifted its share to 22.4% from 18.2%. In China, IDC has Apple second at 17.8% share, up from 15.8%, in a market down 15%. Counterpoint adds that the 18 Pro models sold 12% more units in their first three days in China than the 17 Pro series managed in its entire first week.

The caution sits in the supply chain: the reported October order cut, and lead-time trackers at Bank of America and JPMorgan both finding Pro delivery times shorter than last year. The split launch — only Pro models shipped in September, the Duo follows October 23, and the standard iPhone 18 slips to spring 2027 — makes the signals genuinely ambiguous: some Pro demand may simply be waiting for the foldable. IDC, for its part, expects no smartphone-market recovery before 2028. The Duo's first weekend is the tie-breaker.

November 2: the bar

Apple has confirmed the date on its investor relations page: results at 1:30 p.m. PT on Monday, November 2, with the call at 2:00 p.m. PT hosted by Ternus and CFO Kevan Parekh.

What Apple guided (July 30): revenue growth of 9–11%, implying $111.7–113.7 billion; iPhone revenue growth in the mid-teens; Services growth at a similar pace to Q3's 12%; a foreign-exchange headwind of about 2.5 points; gross margin of 47–48%.

What the Street expects: EPS of $1.99 and revenue of about $113.4 billion (MarketBeat; Barchart). In other words, consensus is already at the top of Apple's own range — there is no cushion priced in. Note that some aggregator pages still show an October 28/29 date with higher estimates; those are stale. The company date is November 2.

Two framing points matter. First, fiscal Q4 (June 28–September 26) captures only launch-week iPhone 18 Pro sales — the Duo lands in fiscal Q1 — so the real output of the print is the holiday-quarter guidance. Second, this is exactly the setup that cost the stock 6–7% in July: a beat against a bar the market had already moved past.

Bull vs. bear

The bull case is an AI-monetization story layered on a premium upgrade cycle. Dan Ives (Yorkville Ives) holds the Street-high $400 target (October 13): Apple has disclosed daily usage limits on server-side Apple Intelligence with expanded access to come for a fee, which Ives reads as a paid Siri AI tier in waiting — potentially adding 15% or more to Services revenue over the next few years, with AI worth roughly $75 a share. Evercore ISI ($380) backs the hardware side with a survey of about 4,000 consumers: 61% intend to buy an iPhone this year, and the implied average selling price is $1,374, up 28%. JPMorgan ($360) and Bank of America ($370) argue the order cuts are cadence, not demand.

The bear case starts with arithmetic: at about 39 times trailing earnings and roughly 35–37 times forward, the stock trades around 30% above its five-year average forward multiple, for a company guiding growth down to 9–11% and margins down to 47–48% — and the consensus target offers no upside from $342. Barclays (Underweight, $245) calls the Services deceleration and China fragility structural; Jefferies (Underperform, about $264) has cut its iPhone ASP growth assumptions; UBS (Neutral) tracks fiscal-Q4 App Store growth at only about 2%, with October facing a tougher comparison and the first full month of the new EU App Store rules in a region that is more than 10% of App Store revenue. Bears also note that the AI story is, so far, rented and unpriced: Western Siri AI runs on Google's Gemini, it does not ship in the EU, it reportedly arrives in China without Siri AI at all, and Apple has announced no AI subscription price or revenue target.

What to watch

  • This weekend: iPhone Duo delivery-time slippage after today's pre-orders — the first clean demand signal of the new cycle.
  • November 2: holiday-quarter guidance above all; Services against a bar now north of $31 billion; Greater China revenue; memory-cost commentary on the call.
  • December: whether the reported China Apple Intelligence launch actually lands, and in what form.
  • Mid-November: Berkshire's Q3 13F — any change to the roughly 228 million-share position, its first filing season with the transition complete.

The takeaway

Apple's execution is not the question — the business just posted a record June quarter and is gaining share in the worst smartphone market on record. The question is the price of the stock. At 39 times earnings, with the average target at the share price and consensus already at the top of guidance, November 2 has to be clean on guidance, Services and China all at once. The foldable's first weekend is the earliest tell.
