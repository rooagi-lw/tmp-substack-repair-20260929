Energy +3.0% — oil's worst week for tankers in a decade sends the whole sector ripping.

Why it's moving

🛢️ Record Hormuz tanker attacks — 10 tankers struck Sept 28–Oct 4, a record weekly high; crossings fell to a two-month low, with just 7 tankers transiting Tuesday (Kpler data, per CNN).

🎯 US strike risk back on — President Trump said overnight he does not want a deal with Iran, as the Pentagon prepares for a possible resumption of large-scale strikes (Reuters Energy Watch, Oct 8; NBC News).

🌀 Hurricane Isaias shuts in 25% of Gulf production — ~500,000 barrels/day offline as Shell and Chevron curtail output and evacuate staff (CNN, Oct 8; Reuters, Oct 6).

📦 US crude stocks fell 3.2M barrels vs a 1.7M-barrel-build consensus, to 424.1M; the IEA agreed Wednesday to accelerate its 100M-barrel emergency release.

💰 Diesel at record prices — AAA national average $6.28/gal vs $3.68 a year ago; European diesel futures closed 6% higher Wednesday.

Driving it

Oil majors + refiners. CVX +3.1% ($211.60), XOM +2.9% ($168.82), MPC +4.7% ($463.21, new 52-week high of $467.71 — Mizuho raised its price target $304 → $457 today). Brent ripped +4.9% to $105.10 before fading to ~$103.63. Meanwhile tech sold off — XLK -2.4%, SOXX -2.3% — as 10-year yields hit 5.35%, the highest since 2002.

Technicals

  • XOM +2.9% — above the 20-day MA ($163.34); support $161, resistance $169.63 then the $176.41 52-week high; hourly uptrend off the Sept 29 lows; gapped up at the open and never filled the gap.
  • CVX +3.1% — reclaimed the 20-day MA ($207.88); support $207.27, resistance $217.77 (52-week high); hourly basing earlier in the week, ripped +0.8% in the first 15 minutes.
  • MPC +4.7% — parabolic, 13% above the 20-day MA ($410.91); RSI 79.3 (overbought); resistance is today's new high $467.71, support $412.76; +10.8% on the week.

Takeaway: Energy is pricing a genuine supply-shock cocktail — war risk in Hormuz, a hurricane in the Gulf, and record diesel — while the rest of the market chokes on 5.35% yields. The question isn't whether oil runs, it's how high before demand destruction bites.
