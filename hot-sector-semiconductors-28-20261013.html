Semiconductors -2.8% — the chip selloff went global overnight: memory names are leading a sixth straight down day for SOXX, even as the Dow climbs on bank earnings.

Why it's moving

  • 🌏 Asia rout overnight: Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell around 5% each in Seoul, dragging the Kospi down as much as 4.8% (per StockTwits/TradingView); IBD reported US-listed SK Hynix (SKHY) dived more than 6% and triggered its 7% sell rule.
  • 💰 AI funding doubts: Broadcom CEO Hock Tan warned at a Singapore conference on Monday that capital and the cost of capital — not chips or power — could be the real constraint on the AI build-out, per CNBC via StockTwits; Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan echoed the return-on-investment concern.
  • 🧠 Memory in the crosshairs: China's CXMT is pushing a denser 4F² DRAM design and a server RDIMM module by year-end — the same competitive threat that hit Micron on Monday, now spreading across the whole memory complex.
  • 🏦 A split tape: Q3 bank earnings from JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo and Citigroup lifted Dow futures +0.8% premarket while Nasdaq-100 futures fell 0.7%, per IBD — money is rotating out of chips, not out of stocks.

Driving it

Memory (DRAM/NAND) is the epicenter, with AI accelerators dragged along:

  • MU (Micron) -5.5% to $934.78
  • SNDK (SanDisk) -3.6% to $1,433.27
  • NVDA (NVIDIA) -1.3% to $223.44

Technicals

  • SNDK -3.6% — below the 20-day MA ($1,694.23) and the 50-day MA ($1,587.95); opened at $1,406.22, session high $1,434.54 — the hourly downtrend is intact and sellers are still in control.

Takeaway

This is now a financing story, not a demand story — until ASML (Wednesday) and TSMC (Thursday) report, the market is pricing the cost of the AI build-out, not its size.
