Semiconductors +1.72% — chips are the one green shoot in a red tape, rallying into month-end as the AI trade reignites ahead of tonight's Micron earnings.

Why it's moving

  • 🧠 Micron reports after the close — Wall Street expects a blockbuster fiscal Q4: $31.56 EPS on $51.2B revenue (AlphaStreet, 33 analysts), roughly 10x last year's $3.03 EPS. JPMorgan reiterated Overweight with a $1,540 target this morning (MoneyCheck). MU is up +1.46% to $1,068.79 into the print.
  • 🚀 HPE +3.24% on a $1.2B deal to supply AI servers to cloud provider Vultr — the first order for its AMD Helios-rack system (Investor's Business Daily). The stock broke past a $63.44 buy point and hit $67.10 intraday, a 3-month high.
  • ⚡ Intel +3.49% and Nebius +1.75% — the AI data-center equipment rotation is broadening: servers, accelerators, and the memory that feeds them are all bid.
  • 💾 The memory supercycle is doing the heavy lifting: conventional DRAM contract prices rose 90–95% quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026 and another 58–63% in Q2, per TrendForce (via Webull).

Driving it

Memory (MU) and AI data-center equipment (HPE, INTC, AMD, AVGO) are carrying the move. The tape: INTC +3.49%, HPE +3.24%, NBIS +1.75%, MU +1.46%, AVGO +1.22%, AMD +0.89%, NVDA +0.84%. Seven of eleven S&P sectors are red — semis are the outlier.

Technicals

  • INTC +3.49% ($120.03) — above the 20-day ($108.74) and 50-day ($100.14); gapped up at the $116.05 open, ran to a $120.41 high, closing near session highs — a reversal bar after a 5-day hourly slide.

Takeaway

Chips are carrying a red tape into month-end — and tonight's Micron print decides whether the memory supercycle keeps paying for it.
