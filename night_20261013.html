US Evening Report · Oct 13

Tuesday, October 13, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Wall Street gave back an early advance and finished fractionally lower on Tuesday, as the first big day of third-quarter earnings produced a mixed verdict on banks and health care, and a second day of selling in memory-chip stocks — sparked by a reported DRAM breakthrough from a Chinese maker — kept technology under pressure. The Dow had risen more than 460 points early in the session before reversing. All eyes now turn to Wednesday's September consumer price index (CPI) report.

The Close

  • S&P 500: 7,805.45, down 1.79 points (-0.02%) — still near record levels (Motley Fool; FNArena)
  • Nasdaq Composite: 27,204.38, down 41.52 points (-0.15%)
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 51,670.10, down 180.45 points (-0.35%)
  • Russell 2000: 2,783.56, down about 0.39% — small caps lagged again

The modest index losses masked sharper moves underneath: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell for a fifth straight session (Thrive in Markets), and bank shares faded after early strength.

Macro & Policy

The story of the day arrives tomorrow. September CPI is due at 8:30 AM ET on Wednesday; economists expect headline prices to rise 0.6% on the month and about 3.6–3.7% from a year earlier (up from 3.4% in August), with core CPI seen up 0.2% on the month and 2.5% on the year (Investrade; Investor's Business Daily).

New York Fed President John Williams told Bloomberg he still sees one more rate hike as appropriate this year, but does not believe another increase will be necessary in 2027, adding that inflation should slow and approach the Fed's 2% target next year (Investrade; Reuters). Markets price roughly a 19% chance of a hike at the Fed's October 28 meeting, with the funds rate currently at 3.75%–4.00% (Morningstar/Dow Jones, citing LSEG). Bank of America's October fund manager survey found 76% of investors expect the Fed to hold this month.

Bond trading resumed after Monday's Columbus Day holiday. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 2.7 basis points to 5.216% (3 PM ET values, Morningstar/Dow Jones Data Talk) — its lowest since September 25, and below last week's 24-year high of 5.365%. The 30-year yield eased to about 5.56–5.57%.

Sectors (all 11 GICS)

Energy led the market, and health care and industrials trailed (Motley Fool). Sector ETF closes (SwingFolio): Energy (XLE) +1.80%, Financials (XLF) -0.42%, Health Care (XLV) -1.35%.

  • Energy — the day's clear leader, carried by firmer crude.
  • Communication Services — led at midday (Motley Fool), helped by Meta Platforms, up 2.63% (SwingFolio).
  • Information Technology — pressured by the memory-chip selloff; the SOX fell a fifth session, though several chip names recovered off intraday lows and AMD turned higher (IBD).
  • Financials — XLF -0.42%; the KBW bank index fell 1.1% as bank earnings faded (SwingFolio).
  • Health Care — XLV -1.35%, the weakest of the verified sector ETFs, dragged by Johnson & Johnson.
  • Industrials — among the day's trailers (Motley Fool).
  • Consumer Discretionary, Consumer Staples, Materials, Real Estate, Utilities — no confirmed closing percentages in the primary sources checked tonight; moves were modest relative to the energy/health-care split that defined the session. Year to date, energy (about +42–46%) and technology (about +32–38%) remain the standout sectors (Reuters; ValuEngine).

Top Movers

Losers:

  • Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) — fell about 6% after mixed results; Motley Fool cited a MedTech sales miss, while Dow Jones noted talc litigation weighed on earnings despite robust sales growth.
  • Domino's Pizza (DPZ) — down 4.76% on weaker US sales growth (SwingFolio).
  • Blackstone (BX) — down 4.18% as rising yields and analyst target cuts weighed (Motley Fool).
  • Micron Technology (MU) — down 3.62% as the CXMT DRAM story hit memory names for a second day (Motley Fool); Western Digital fell 1.6% and SK hynix about 3% (ShareCafe).
  • Wells Fargo (WFC) — down about 3% on a mixed print (revenue/NII miss); JPMorgan (JPM) fell 0.9% after earnings and revenue missed despite a raised 2026 net-interest-income guide; Goldman Sachs (GS) slipped about 0.2% despite stronger-than-expected revenue (ShareCafe; Investrade). Bank of America (BAC) fell 1.14% in regular trade.

Gainers / notable:

  • Meta Platforms (META) — up 2.63%, propping up the major indexes (SwingFolio; IBD).
  • UnitedHealth (UNH) — adjusted EPS of $4.69 beat the $4.14 consensus on $112.7 billion of revenue, and management raised its 2026 adjusted EPS floor to at least $20.50 (Stocktwits/Fiscal.ai; Schaeffer's). Unusual volume: about 12.6 million shares versus a 7.4 million average (MarketBeat).

Technicals & Sentiment

Asia-Pacific & Europe

Tuesday's Asia-Pacific session set the day's tone: South Korea's Kospi plunged about 5.5–5.8% on memory-chip valuation and competition fears, Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.93–2.63% across reports, and Taiwan's weighted index dropped 1.99%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng gained 0.20% and Singapore's Straits Times rose 0.28% (Hindu BusinessLine; IC Markets). India's Sensex jumped 1.54% to 73,033.78 and the Nifty 50 gained 1.60% on an IT rally. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 finished essentially flat at 8,783.90 (FNArena). In Europe, the FTSE 100 eased 0.06% to 10,650.33, the DAX slipped 0.07%, and the Euro Stoxx 50 fell 0.55%.

Early Wednesday in Asia, the chip selloff was easing: MSCI's Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index rose 0.5%, the Nikkei added 0.2%, and the Kospi rebounded 1.8%, with Samsung Electronics and SK hynix up more than 2% (Reuters).

US Futures Overnight

Overnight futures were little changed heading into CPI day: Dow futures roughly flat, S&P 500 futures up 0.1%, and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.2% (Investor's Business Daily). Reuters put S&P 500 e-minis up 0.04% at 7,858.8 in early Asia trade. ASX 200 futures pointed down 30 points (-0.34%) to 8,788 (FNArena).

Next-Day Outlook & Economic Calendar — Wednesday, October 14

  • 8:30 AM ET — September CPI (Labor Department). Consensus: headline +0.6% m/m, +3.6–3.7% y/y; core +0.2% m/m, +2.5% y/y. A hot print would test the market's assumption that the Fed can hold on October 28; a soft core reading would relieve the pressure that has kept the 10-year above 5%.
  • Earnings before the open: Morgan Stanley (MS), Bank of America (BAC), State Street (STT), BlackRock (BLK) and ASML (ASML) (Investrade; IBD).
