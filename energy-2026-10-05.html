🔥 Energy +1.15% — oil stocks climbed while crude itself fell, a rare split.

Why it's moving

  • 🛢️ Crude sliding, stocks rising: WTI fell about 1% to $90.21 and Brent slipped to around $101.30, yet XLE rose 1.15% — only the sixth time since the U.S.-Iran war began that the energy ETF gained more than 1% while crude fell, per MarketWatch analysis of FactSet data.
  • 📦 Supply relief: G7 nations agreed Friday to release 100 million barrels of crude and diesel from strategic reserves, and Middle East exports climbed above pre-war levels on four of seven days last week, according to shipping data cited by CNBC.
  • 🏭 Refining bonanza: Barclays notes the benchmark refining margin is running at unseen levels near $40 a barrel — refiners profit as cheap crude becomes cheap input.
  • ⚖️ Policy: President Trump ruled out a diesel export ban that would have tightened product markets, and Saudi Aramco cut November official selling prices for Asian buyers by $3 a barrel to six-year lows, per WSJ Market Talk.

Driving it

Refiners and oilfield services are doing the heavy lifting. Valero (VLO) +0.49% to $408.30 (Zacks BATS quote, 9:50am ET), Halliburton (HAL) +3.8% to $33.05, Baker Hughes (BKR) +3.3% to $57.83. The majors joined in: ExxonMobil (XOM) +0.1% to $164.12 and Chevron (CVX) +0.05% to $206.79 — 19 of XLE's 21 components were up, per MarketWatch.

Technicals

  • XLE: dipped to $62.05 at 9:45am, then drove to a $63.68 session high at 12:16pm and consolidated near $63.55. Hourly: steady climb 9–11am, flat since noon. Holds above the 50-day MA at $62.20; the 52-week high $66.17 is next resistance.
  • XOM: above the 50-day MA $160.61; support $160.6, resistance $176.41 (52-week high); +19.5% over the past 90 days.
  • CVX: above the 50-day MA $202.83; support $202.8, resistance $217.78 (52-week high).
  • VLO: far above the 50-day MA $356.53; resistance $419.04 (52-week high).
  • HAL: up 3.8% but still under the 50-day MA $34.06 — that line is now first resistance.

Takeaway

Cheap crude used to mean cheap energy stocks — today it meant record refining margins and a bid for the whole sector.
