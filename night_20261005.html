US Evening Report · Oct 5

Monday, October 5, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Market close

US stocks closed higher on Monday, led by technology. The S&P 500 finished at 7,773.95, up 0.66%, sitting within about 0.3% of its August 13 record close, according to Investopedia. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.05% to 27,477.31, a new record close — its 23rd of 2026, per Barron's. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.18% to 51,267.90.

Friday's closes for reference: S&P 7,722.72 (+0.73%), Nasdaq 27,190.86 (+1.19%), Dow 51,176.96 (+0.49%).

Macro and policy

The Fed's September 16 rate hike — a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00%, the first increase in three years — continues to hang over the market. The September jobs report released Friday came in soft: only 29,000 payrolls added versus roughly 84,000 expected, with unemployment at 4.2% and wage growth cooling to 3.0% year over year, the weakest since May 2021. CME FedWatch pricing now implies only about a 24% chance of a follow-up hike at the October 27–28 meeting, down from 71% a week ago, though a December move remains roughly 86–87% priced in.

Monday's data: the ISM services PMI printed 54.9 for September, with the prices-paid component at 74.0 — the highest since July 2022. Second-quarter GDP was revised up from 1.5% to 2.2%.

On energy, the G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels from emergency stockpiles, sending crude lower: Brent fell 1.9% to $100.32 and WTI dropped 1.8% to $89.43. Aramco CEO Amin Nasser warned that Middle East conflict has depleted inventories and rebuilding could take two years.

Geopolitics stayed on traders' screens: the Iran war and broader Middle East conflict continue, Iran's parliament speaker said the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until US conditions are met, and Yemen's Saudi-backed government launched a campaign against the Houthis.

In Europe, the euro fell to a 17-month low against the dollar near $1.116, French bonds sold off on budget woes, and Spain's Prime Minister Sánchez called a snap election. In Brazil, Flávio Bolsonaro's first-round victory over Lula sent stocks jumping and the real rallying more than 4%.

Sector performance

All 11 GICS sectors finished in the green at the close except one, according to Investopedia. Information Technology led the way to a record close, with Communication Services also among the leaders and Energy firm. Real Estate was the sole laggard of the day.

Top movers

  • PTC surged 33.5%, the best S&P 500 performer of the day, after Schneider Electric agreed to a $22.6 billion all-cash acquisition at $205 per share — about a 42% premium to Friday's close (Barron's). Autodesk rose 3.8% on the industrial-software valuation reassessment.
  • C.H. Robinson fell 10.9%, the biggest S&P 500 loser, after announcing a $5.8 billion cash-and-stock acquisition of RXO; RXO jumped 21.8%.
  • Nvidia gained 2.1% to a record $238.90, lifting its market capitalization to $5.76 trillion. TSMC's ADR rose 2.75%, briefly pushing the company above $2.5 trillion in value.
  • Tesla added more than 2%, Broadcom more than 2%, Meta 1.9%, and Microsoft more than 1%. Western Digital climbed 6% and Seagate 4%.
  • Lagging chip names: Intel, Qualcomm, Arm, ASML, and AMD all closed lower, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor index was roughly flat.
  • Market breadth remained poor: it was the 15th straight session with more new lows than new highs, and only about 20% of Russell 3000 constituents are above their 50-day moving average (Fundstrat).

Technicals and sentiment

The VIX closed at 15.52, up 1.37%, after spiking above 16 in early trading before fading (Barron's). The 10-year Treasury yield climbed 3.4 basis points to 5.310%, its highest close since April 2002, per the WSJ and MarketWatch. The 30-year closed at 5.664%, the highest since May 2002, and the 2-year at 4.831%.

Gold spot ended near $4,134 an ounce, down 0.2%; December futures settled 0.1% lower at $4,156.80. Silver fell 0.6% to $60.67.

Asia-Pacific overnight

Tuesday morning trading in Asia was modestly higher. Japan's Nikkei 225 gained about 0.7%, the MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index rose 0.2%, and Australia's ASX 200 was up roughly 0.3%–0.6%, per Reuters. Hang Seng futures pointed to a stronger open around 24,266; Korea's KOSPI was near flat. Mainland Chinese markets were closed for the National Day holiday. India's Sensex closed Monday up 0.66% at 72,382.47.

US futures overnight

Overnight index futures were near the flatline: S&P 500 futures up 0.1%, Dow futures up about 51 points (0.1%), and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.2%, per Monday-evening trading.
