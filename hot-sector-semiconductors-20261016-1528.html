Why it's moving

  • 💰 CoreWeave (CRWV) -12.4% led the scare: according to The Motley Fool, its 5-year credit default swaps hit a record ~1,195 basis points and some bonds trade near 79% of face value, with about $52 billion in loans and lease obligations.
  • 📉 According to Reuters, the yield on CoreWeave's October 2031 bond briefly surged past 16% on October 15, and its shares fell more than 8% in early trading today, set for their worst week in over 11 months if losses hold.
  • ⚖️ Morningstar cut its price targets on CoreWeave and IREN, citing rising neocloud debt costs and pressure from hyperscalers raising debt for their own expansion, per Stocktwits.
  • 🔻 The selling is broad, not single-name: SOXX is down 11.66% over 5 sessions per Finnhub, and XLK Technology fell 2.73% today while defensive and value sectors held up — XLE Energy rose 2.22%.

Driving it

The driving sub-segment is financing-sensitive AI hardware — memory, logic chips and the neocloud supply chain — not self-funding mega-caps. Micron (MU) -4.8%, Intel (INTC) -5.4%, AMD -5.0% and Marvell (MRVL) -4.5% fell hard, while NVIDIA (NVDA) -0.9%, which funds expansion from operating cash, held up far better.

Takeaway: This is a financing story, not a demand story — until credit markets calm on AI debt, chips that need borrowed money to grow will keep taking the hardest hits.
