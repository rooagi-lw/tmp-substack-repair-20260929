Deep Dive: TSMC — Record Profit, Raised Guidance, and a Cautious Note on 2027

TSMC just posted the strongest quarter in its history — and its stock still fell, because this market is no longer pricing chip demand; it is pricing the cost of funding it.

What happened

  • 🏭 TSMC reported third-quarter results on Thursday, October 15: net income of NT$763.06 billion, up 68.7% year over year — a record for the sixth straight quarter, according to Reuters.
  • 💰 Revenue reached NT$1.49 trillion (US$46.72 billion), beating the NT$746.3 billion profit estimate and US$45.67 billion sales estimate from analysts polled by LSEG and FactSet; earnings were US$4.60 per ADR versus US$4.46 expected, per Investor's Business Daily.
  • 📈 TSMC raised the bar for Q4: revenue guidance of US$52.3–54.3 billion, a midpoint of US$53.3 billion versus consensus of US$50.22 billion, per IBD.
  • ⚠️ But per Reuters, TSMC struck a cautious note for 2027, citing macroeconomic uncertainties — and TSM fell about 3.6% to $414.40 in early Thursday trading after sliding 3.4% on Wednesday.

How we got here

  • 🔥 Wednesday's hot US inflation print — September CPI at 3.7% versus 3.6% expected — pushed market-implied odds of a Fed rate hike at the October 28 meeting up to roughly 44–49%, and the Nasdaq and S&P 500 posted their biggest one-day drops since July.
  • 💳 AI financing stress moved to center stage: CoreWeave's bonds pushed toward 15% yields as its stock plunged 15% on Wednesday, and Oracle fell nearly 9% — together with chipmakers and hyperscalers, the group wiped out about $80 billion of market value in one session, according to Reuters.
  • 🇰🇷 The selloff went global overnight: South Korea's KOSPI closed down 7.57% at a six-month low after a circuit-breaker halt, with Samsung Electronics -9.5% and SK Hynix -11.1%, per Reuters.
  • 📉 The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had already fallen for six straight sessions through Wednesday, closing below its 50-day moving average and down 11.6% since its October 6 close, per IBD.

By the numbers

  • 68% of TSMC's Q3 revenue came from high-performance computing, per the company's reported mix.
  • Advanced technologies of 7nm and below were 79% of wafer revenue: 3nm 33%, 5nm 28%, and 2nm 8% — 2nm already up from 3% in Q2.
  • Gross margin was 66.4%, down from 67.7% in Q2, and Q4 guidance of 65–67% reflects the 2nm ramp and overseas fab costs.
  • TSMC reiterated a 2026 capital budget of US$60–64 billion and now expects full-year 2026 revenue growth of about 44% in US dollar terms, per its guidance.
  • The stock sits well below its all-time high of $487.47 set on October 5, per IBD MarketSurge.

Why it matters

TSMC is the one company that sees the whole AI build-out — every major AI accelerator from Nvidia, AMD and Apple runs through its fabs. When it says leading-edge demand still exceeds supply and guides Q4 revenue 14% higher sequentially, the demand side of the AI trade is intact. What is breaking is the financing side: with CPI at 3.7% and hike odds rising, the discount rate on every future AI dollar just went up, and leveraged buyers like CoreWeave are being repriced first. TSMC's cautious 2027 note matters because it is the first hint that the foundry can see that financing wall from where it sits.

Bull vs. bear

Bull: The order book says the boom is real. TSMC guided above consensus, ASML just raised its full-year outlook with customers already discussing orders for 2029 and 2030, according to Aju Business Daily, and Jefferies' Mohit Kumar told Reuters he expects earnings season to show demand remains robust. J.P.Morgan's Mixo Das added that AI stocks could be substantially higher within 6 to 12 months, per Reuters.

Bear: The guide cannot fix the rate path. Gross margin is diluting as 2nm ramps, TSMC itself flagged 2027 macro uncertainty, and if AI infrastructure funding stays at 15% for the marginal buyer, capex plans get trimmed long before TSMC's current backlog does. A stock down from $487 to $414 in eight sessions is the market voting that financing wins.

What to watch next

  • 📊 Today's US data: producer prices, retail sales and weekly jobless claims, per Investopedia's morning calendar.
  • 🏦 The October 28 Fed meeting: whether hike odds near 44–49% keep climbing.
  • ☁️ Hyperscaler earnings later this month: capex guidance is the next real test of the financing story.
  • 💾 Memory's response: UBS argues the CXMT threat to Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix is overblown for now, per Barron's — watch whether memory names stabilize first.

Takeaway

TSMC just proved AI demand is not the problem — the cost of money is, and until that changes, even record earnings get sold.
