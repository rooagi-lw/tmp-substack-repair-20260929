Consumer Staples -1.5% — the safety trade is unraveling

The market's hiding place just stopped hiding. Consumer staples are the worst-performing sector this morning, down 1.5% to $80.60, as a historic bond selloff forces a violent rotation out of defensives.

Why it's moving

  • 💸 Yields at 2002 highs: The 10-year Treasury hit 5.32% and the 30-year 5.66% — their highest since 2002 — after Treasuries logged their worst quarter since 1994, according to Reuters. When risk-free money pays that much, dividend defensives lose their bid.
  • 🔄 Two-day defensive dump: Staples, healthcare, industrials, financials, and real estate all fell more than 1% yesterday, per Yonhap Infomax — and this morning the selling is spreading, with XLV -1.4%, XLI -1.3%, XLF -1.1%, and XLRE -1.0%.
  • 🚀 Money rotating into tech: Micron's earnings beat — with guidance above expectations and $32 billion in supply commitments — plus Alphabet's Gemini 4 launch (+2.3% premarket) are pulling capital toward the AI trade, per Reuters.
  • 📉 Fear creeping up: The VIX touched 16.84, a two-week high, as Dow futures slid to a three-month low, according to Reuters.

Driving it

The selloff is broad-based across staples — retail, household products, and beverages are all red. The four heaviest holdings:

  • WMT -2.7% to $103.92 (Walmart, 10.7% of XLP)
  • PG -2.1% to $145.28 (Procter & Gamble, 7.8%)
  • COST -1.5% to $910.34 (Costco, 9.2%)
  • KO -0.9% to $86.08 (Coca-Cola, 7.6%)

All prices as of 9:34 AM ET, via Finnhub.

Technicals

  • WMT -2.7% — Below the 20-day ($107.39) and 50-day ($109.27) MAs; broke down -3.2% on Sep 24, the bounce failed, now testing the 30-day low near $102 support. Opened $106.97, slid all morning to $103.92.
  • COST -1.5% — Slipped back under the 20-day MA ($910.11); 50-day at $933.86 overhead. Support $883, resistance $926–931. Opened $924.61, faded to $910.34.
  • PG -2.1% — Below both MAs (20-day $146.42, 50-day $145.94); Sep 28's +2.2% bounce fully erased. Support $141.86, resistance $149.48.
  • KO -0.9% — Below both MAs (20-day $88.06, 50-day $87.69); sitting right on the 30-day low of $86.56 after five straight down sessions.

Takeaway

When bonds pay 5.3%, nobody pays up for safety — the defensive trade is unraveling in real time.
