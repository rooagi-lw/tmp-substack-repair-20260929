US Evening Report · Sep 30

Wednesday, September 30, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET. All data below are official closing snapshots, not intraday values.

US Market Close Recap

Wall Street ended September with a split decision: cooler-than-expected inflation data lifted stocks early, but the relief faded as Treasury yields punched to multi-decade highs. All three major indexes climbed after the August PCE print, then gave back most of the gains in the final hour — the Dow fell roughly 450 points, having opened higher and traded nearly 600 points above its close at the peak.

The S&P 500 turned lower and the Dow touched session lows in the final minutes. Small caps also faded: the Russell 2000 gave up early gains to lose 0.4%, and the S&P MidCap 400 fell 0.6% to 3,596. The Dow snapped a five-month winning streak with its worst month since June; the S&P fell for the third month in four, while the Nasdaq notched its second straight monthly gain. For the quarter, both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq recorded their second straight quarterly advances.

Macro & Policy

  • August PCE inflation came in cooler than expected. Headline PCE rose 3.4% year over year in August, below the 3.7% economists expected, with a 0.3% monthly rise. Core PCE rose 3.0% year over year, down from 3.3% in July and below the 3.3% estimate, with a 0.2% monthly gain. The softer print cut the odds of a quarter-point Fed hike at the October meeting to about 37%, down from roughly 51% the prior session and nearly 71% a week ago (CME FedWatch).
  • Q2 GDP revised higher. The Commerce Department's third estimate put second-quarter GDP growth at 2.2% annualized, up from 1.5%, on stronger consumer spending and investment, including AI-infrastructure spending.
  • Private hiring rebounded in September. ADP reported private payrolls grew by 90,000 jobs, up from a downwardly revised 36,000 in August and above the 70,000 consensus. Education and health added 55,000 jobs; leisure and hospitality added 22,000; financial activities lost 16,000. Base pay rose 3.2% year over year.
  • Fed stance stays restrictive. The Fed raised rates on September 16 — its first increase since 2023 — and markets now price several hikes over the next 12 months. New York Fed President John Williams said there was no urgency to act at the October meeting; Governor Michael Barr flagged that more hikes could still be needed. Tuesday's data showed consumer confidence sinking to its lowest since 2014 and JOLTS openings slipping to 7.08 million.
  • Shutdown risk off the table. The House passed a funding stopgap through December 11, removing the government-shutdown overhang.
  • Energy policy in motion. The Department of Energy issued an RFP for an exchange of up to 40 million barrels of crude from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, part of a 172-million-barrel release announced by President Trump in coordination with IEA members' 400-million-barrel commitment. Saudi Arabia resumed tanker loadings from Yanbu, easing near-term supply pressure, though US-Iran peace talks remain stalled after seven months of war.

Sector Performance

Information Technology and Communication Services were the top-performing S&P 500 sectors on Wednesday, with the tech index up 0.6% as megacap and growth names — Microsoft, Apple, Nvidia — ticked higher. Nine of the 11 major S&P sectors ended the day lower, with utilities, energy, and semiconductor shares swinging the session as the yield surge pressured rate-sensitive groups.

Top Movers

  • Micron (MU) — the day's centerpiece. Shares closed essentially flat at $1,065 in the regular session, then reported fiscal Q4 after the close: revenue of $54.23 billion and adjusted EPS of $33.42, both well above views, with next-quarter guidance of $61.5 billion in revenue and $38.15 EPS — far ahead of the roughly $51.1 billion / $31.52 consensus. The stock edged higher overnight, though management warned profit margins will narrow. Strategists asked whether the market is now closer to peak memory shortage.
  • Alphabet (GOOGL) — rose more than 1% after unveiling its Gemini 4 Argon AI model, touted for major gains in coding, cybersecurity, and professional applications.
  • Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) — broke out on bullish guidance and a large order for systems featuring AMD's new Helios AI rack and chips; Palo Alto Networks (PANW), Snowflake (SNOW), and Microsoft (MSFT) also flashed buy signals on a strong day for software and megacaps.
  • Liquidia (LQDA) — took a heavy hit over a patent dispute, one of the session's worst performers.
  • Bloom Energy (BE) — carried over a 12%+ surge toward $300 from Tuesday's session.
  • Carnival (CCL) — up nearly 12% after reporting record $8.44 billion revenue and raising its full-year guide; Fair Isaac (FICO) — down 21.6% after the FHFA added VantageScore to the Fannie/Freddie pricing grid.

Technicals & Sentiment

  • VIX: 15.86, down 1.31% — the fear gauge stayed calm under 16 even as yields surged, a gap worth watching.
  • 10-year Treasury yield: 5.29% at the close, the highest since May 2002, with an intraday high of 5.306%. The quarter's 0.87-point rise was the steepest since Q1 1994. The real 10-year yield is a hair's breadth from 3%.

  • 2-year Treasury yield: around 4.89%.

  • Gold: back above $4,180, up about 0.5% after Monday's brutal 4% slide, resilient despite the yield headwind.
  • WTI crude: about $90.65, up roughly 1%; Brent held near $98 as markets weighed a possible West Asian supply recovery.
  • Dollar: DXY near 101.47; USD/JPY steady at 157.4; EUR/USD above 1.1350; sterling under 1.3250; AUD/USD below 0.70 even after the RBA hiked to 4.60%.

Asia-Pacific Markets

Asian markets opened October with little direction as the late Wall Street reversal offset the softer US inflation data.

  • Japan: Nikkei 225 up about 1%, led by chip shares (Tokyo Electron, Advantest) as semiconductor strength offset losses in financials.
  • Hong Kong and Shanghai: closed for the National Day / Golden Week holiday (October 1–7); Hang Seng futures eased 170 points in night trading, and Stock Connect southbound trading is suspended through October 7.
  • South Korea: KOSPI slightly lower as foreigners sold tech; Taiwan's TAIEX had closed up 0.65% at 47,940, with TSMC futures firm after hours.
  • Australia: ASX 200 futures pointed to a weak October start, down about 1.6% in early trade, after a 0.92% gain the prior session.
  • India: GIFT Nifty down 0.33% at 22,580, signaling a soft open.
  • MSCI's broadest Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index was down about 0.2%. The Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index rose 0.6% to 5,696.

US Futures Overnight

US futures pointed slightly higher into the new month: Dow futures up about 0.1%, S&P 500 futures up about 0.3%, and Nasdaq-100 futures up about 0.5%, supported by the Micron beat and Alphabet's AI launch. Overnight moves don't necessarily carry into the regular session.
