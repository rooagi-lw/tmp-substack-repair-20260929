US Evening Report · Oct 6

Tuesday, October 6, 2026 — closing levels as of 4:00 PM ET

Market close

US stocks notched fresh records on Tuesday. The S&P 500 climbed 0.58% (44.98 points) to 7,818.93 — its first close above 7,800 and the 28th record close of 2026, according to the WSJ citing Dow Jones Market Data. The Nasdaq Composite rose 0.45% (122.48 points) to 27,599.79, a second straight record, per Morningstar/Dow Jones Market Data. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.49% (253.38 points) to 51,521.28. The small-cap Russell 2000 diverged, slipping 0.59% to 2,830.44, per vistapglobal.

Monday's closes for reference: S&P 7,773.95 (+0.66%), Nasdaq 27,477.31 (+1.05%), Dow 51,267.90 (+0.18%).

Macro and policy

Bond-market relief set the tone: the 10-year Treasury yield fell about 4 basis points to 5.27%, retreating from Monday's 24-year high above 5.35%, per the WSJ; the 2-year sat at 4.789% and the 30-year at 5.641%. French budget fears receded, steadying the euro, the WSJ reported.

The August trade deficit, reported at 8:30 AM ET, widened to $105.6 billion from a revised $92.8 billion in July — the largest monthly gap since March 2025 and above the $102 billion consensus, per tradingnews.com. Equities took the constructive read: capital-goods imports tied to AI investment, with the Atlanta Fed's Q3 GDP tracker still at 3.7% annualized.

Fed pricing cooled on further hikes. September's FOMC minutes — from the September 15–16 meeting that raised rates a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% — land Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET. After September payrolls added just 29,000 and unemployment held at 4.2%, CME FedWatch put October hold odds near 76–78%, while a December hike remains roughly 87% priced, per Reuters. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said Tuesday that more hikes could be needed if inflationary shocks from energy prices, tariffs, or AI demand prove persistent; New York Fed President John Williams, speaking the same day, repeated there is "no need for urgency."

The AI trade broadened beyond tech: Nvidia set its third straight intraday record and closed up 0.1%, closing in on a $6 trillion market capitalization, per Investopedia. SpaceX added 0.5% after Monday's 7.5% jump.

On energy and geopolitics, WTI settled near $89.44 a barrel and Brent near $101, per the WSJ and Reuters. Non-Iran Gulf oil flows surged past 81% of pre-war September levels despite Houthi attacks on Saudi targets, while the US blockade holds Iranian exports at zero, Reuters reported. Overnight, Jair Bolsonaro won Brazil's general election — the Bovespa jumped 8% and the Brazil ETF EWZ 12%, per thehindubusinessline.

Sector performance

Ten of the 11 GICS sectors finished higher, per Barron's. Utilities was the runaway leader, up 3.0% — its largest single-day gain in five months — powered by Constellation Energy's 20-year nuclear power purchase agreement with Alphabet and strength in Vistra. Barron's noted it was only the third time since 1990 that utilities led the market on a day the S&P 500 hit a record. Consumer Discretionary rose 1.4%, per Reuters. Real Estate added about 1.1%, Industrials 0.87%, and Consumer Staples 0.86% (XLRE/XLI/XLP closing proxies), Technology 0.53% (XLK), Energy 0.47% (XLE), Materials 0.46% (XLB), Financials 0.24% (XLF), and Communication Services 0.04% (XLC). Health Care was the lone decliner, down 0.2%.

Top movers

  • Ciena (CIEN) surged 13.9%, Barron's "Hot Stock" of the day.
  • Constellation Energy (CEG) jumped 12.2–12.3% after signing a 20-year nuclear power purchase agreement with Alphabet, per IBD and Barron's. Vistra also ranked among the S&P 500's top gainers, per Barron's.
  • Astera Labs (ALAB) gained 7.6% and Nebius (NBIS) 7.4%, per IBD.
  • Cisco (CSCO) rose 4.5%, the strongest blue-chip performer of the day, per IBD. Walmart, Amazon, and Home Depot each added roughly 2%.
  • Seagate Technology (STX) fell 9.2%, the S&P 500's biggest loser, per Barron's. Salesforce (CRM) lost 2.1%, per IBD.

Technicals and sentiment

The VIX closed at 15.06, down about 3%, per vistapglobal. The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 5.270%, down 4 basis points from Monday's 24-year high, per the WSJ.

Gold December futures settled $30.80 higher (+0.75%) at $4,159.20 an ounce, per the WSJ; spot gold traded near $4,160–$4,170, up about 0.7%, per Reuters.

Asia-Pacific overnight

Asia's Tuesday session (local October 6) closed before the US open. Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 1.05% to 70,683.98, breaking above 70,000 after Monday's 2.40% surge, per SKN. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1.00% to 24,280.56, back above 24,000. Korea's KOSPI slipped 0.89%, one of the region's few decliners. Australia's ASX 200 added 0.54%. Mainland Chinese markets were closed for the National Day Golden Week holiday (October 1–7) and reopen October 8.

Early Wednesday trading in Asia was mixed: the ASX 200 was down 0.32%, the Nikkei up 0.07%, and the KOSPI near flat (−0.02%) at the open, per scanx.trade.

US futures overnight

Overnight index futures were near the flatline: S&P 500 futures up a fraction, Nasdaq-100 futures down 0.1%, and Dow futures down 0.1%, per Investors.com.
