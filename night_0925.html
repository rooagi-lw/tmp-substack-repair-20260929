The day's conclusion on the whole US market — Friday, September 25, 2026. All index values are official closing levels (4:00 p.m. ET).

Market close: green Friday to end a volatile week

Wall Street closed a turbulent week on its highs. A pause in the global bond selloff and a sharp pullback in oil — on fresh signs the US and Iran are talking about reopening the Strait of Hormuz — gave stocks room to run.

  • S&P 500: 7,743.41, up 39.28 points (+0.5%)
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 51,828.62, up 478.64 points (+0.9%)
  • Nasdaq Composite: 27,068.72, up 129.34 points (+0.5%)
  • Russell 2000: 2,837.55, up 1.97 points (+0.1%)

For the week: the S&P 500 gained 1.2%, the Nasdaq rose 2.1%, and the Dow added 0.3% — snapping a three-week losing streak. The indexes finished the week higher despite a midweek selloff, and all three remain near record highs.

Macro & policy: Hormuz diplomacy, a bond breather, a firm Fed

The day's biggest cross-asset move was in oil. Reuters reported that US and Iranian negotiators are discussing a phased deal — Tehran reopens the Strait of Hormuz, Washington eases its economic blockade — while the FT reported Iran offered a 7-day ceasefire proposal, and President Pezeshkian said in an interview that the Houthis are responsible for their own actions. Brent fell about 2% to ~$104.4 a barrel and WTI dropped roughly 2% to ~$92.7, partially unwinding the week's spike. Saudi crude exports reached 6 million barrels a day despite the damaged East-West pipeline staying shut.

Bonds got a breather too. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 5.16–5.18% in late trading, down ~4 bps on the day, after touching 5.228% intraday Thursday — its highest since 2007. The 30-year settled near 5.5% after breaking above that level for the first time in 22 years; the 2-year edged down to ~4.86%.

The Fed drumbeat stays hawkish. New York Fed President Williams said another rate hike before year-end "looks reasonable," and futures were pricing about a 70% chance of a hike at the October 28 FOMC meeting. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack warned that persistently high inflation is starting to change how consumers and businesses think about longer-term costs.

On the data front: August durable goods orders were unchanged ($338.6B; economists expected −0.3%), up 8.4% from a year earlier. The University of Michigan's final September consumer sentiment reading came in at 48.1, down from 51.7 in August — softer, but better than the preliminary estimate, while long-run inflation expectations moved higher.

On diplomacy: the Trump–Xi Washington summit was heavy on ceremony and light on outcomes — major disagreements on trade, technology, and Beijing's support for Iran — though more than 30 CEOs were invited to the state dinner. The US–China trade truce was extended to January 10.

Sectors: technology leads Friday; tech and healthcare take the week

Technology led Friday's advance as AI enthusiasm offset rate pressure, while energy lagged as crude pulled back on the Hormuz diplomacy.

For the week, four of the eleven sectors finished higher: technology (+3.1%) and healthcare (+1.6%) led, while energy (−3.1%) and utilities (−3.0%) were the worst. Market breadth stayed narrow — the gains were concentrated in big tech and software, with the average stock lagging and value down on the week.

Asia-Pacific: Tokyo higher; much of the region on holiday

Japan's Nikkei 225 closed Friday at 66,364.2, up about 1.3% on chip and AI strength. Elsewhere the region was largely shut: mainland China, Taiwan, and South Korea were all closed for the Mid-Autumn Festival/Chuseok, with Stock Connect and overnight futures sessions suspended. Hong Kong night-session futures for the Hang Seng slipped 21 points to 24,501; the NASDAQ Golden Dragon China Index fell 0.6% to 5,687, with Alibaba's US shares down ~0.7% and Tencent roughly flat.

US futures overnight

The week ahead & the calendar

Monday opens with futures reopening Sunday evening, then a heavy calendar builds through the week: August PCE inflation on Wednesday, alongside Micron's quarterly results; ISM manufacturing on Thursday; and the September jobs report on Friday, October 2. Every one of them will feed October hike odds, with the 10-year above 5.1% keeping valuation pressure on.

The bottom line: stocks closed a wild week in the green, but the setup hasn't changed — AI enthusiasm and energy diplomacy carried Friday, while the bond market still demands a 5%-plus risk-free rate. Next week's inflation and jobs data decide whether the Fed hikes again in October.
