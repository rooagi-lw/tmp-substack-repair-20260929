Deep Dive: Humana — The $1.5 Billion Star Turn

One CMS spreadsheet, published Thursday evening, just re-rated Humana's earnings power through 2028 — and the stock's 12.8% surge to a new 52-week high is only the down payment.

What happened

📋 The release — Late Thursday, October 8, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services published the 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings, the 1-to-5 quality scorecard that decides which insurers earn extra federal bonus payments, per Reuters.

⭐ Humana's comeback — Humana's largest Medicare Advantage contract, covering 2.4 million members, rose from 3.5 stars back to 4 stars, putting it back above the bonus threshold, according to CMS data reported by TradingView/Stocktwits. Humana said 95% of its Medicare Advantage members will be in plans rated four stars or higher in 2027 — up from 25% for 2025 per Stocktwits, and far above the 60–70% J.P. Morgan had expected, per Reuters.

📈 The market verdict — HUM surged as much as 13% in after-hours trading Thursday (TradingView), then jumped 14–16% in premarket Friday, hitting about $442.50 before the open, per Investor's Business Daily. Baird upgraded Humana to Outperform and raised its price target to $596 from $390, citing confidence in more than $35 in adjusted EPS in 2028; Barclays raised its target to $582 from $515, keeping Overweight and pointing to a 93% bonus mix, per The Fly.

📉 The other side of the ledger — The same ratings crushed losers: CVS Health fell about 1.2% after three contracts were downgraded to 3.5 stars (IBD), Alignment Healthcare plunged 23% after its largest California contract fell below the cutoff (TradingView), while Clover Health rose 3.4% after its PPO plans earned the top five-star rating (IBD, finnhub).

How we got here

October 2024 — the fall. The last time CMS published ratings, Humana's share of members in 4-star-or-better plans collapsed from 94% to 25% after a large contract dropped from 4.5 stars to 3.5 — cutting the bonus payments Humana was set to receive in 2026, per TradingView.

The 2026 hangover. That lost bonus is the reason Humana guides full-year 2026 adjusted EPS to "at least $9.00" versus $17.14 in 2025 — the decline explicitly reflects the star-ratings headwind for Bonus Year 2026, net of mitigation, per RTTNews.

2025–2026 — the fix. Management spent a year rebuilding the metric. Evercore ISI analyst Elizabeth Anderson said Humana improved in drug-plan quality, health-plan quality, and readmissions, reflecting "the importance management has placed on fixing this metric over the past year," per Reuters.

July 29, 2026 — margin over volume. Humana beat Q2 estimates (adjusted EPS $7.61 vs. $7.22 expected; revenue $40.87 billion, up 26.2%), yet the stock fell 6.8% that day as management announced it would exit plans covering about 600,000 members — 9.3% of its 6.454 million individual MA base — for 2027, aiming for a stable pretax Medicare Advantage margin of at least 3% by 2028, per TS2 and the company's 8-K.

September 25, 2026 — the setup. Barclays pre-positioned: upgraded Humana to Overweight, raised its target to $515, and estimated the H5216 contract alone is worth $1.5 billion of EBIT and $9.00 of EPS if it reaches bonus status — raising the probability to 75%, per Business Insider/Investing.com. Thursday's release was the test.

By the numbers

The stakes are enormous because bonus payments scale with membership. Crossing 4 stars unlocks bonus payments that raise the federal money an insurer receives per member — and a single half-star move on a large contract can be worth hundreds of millions of dollars, per TradingView. Overall, about 71% of Medicare Advantage prescription-drug plan enrollees will be in contracts rated four stars or higher in 2027, the health department said, per Reuters — and Humana is now near the top of that distribution at 95%.

The company behind the number: Q2 2026 revenue of $40.87 billion (per Humana's earnings release), full-year 2026 revenue guidance of at least $160 billion — with the Insurance segment at least $155 billion — and individual Medicare Advantage membership of 6.454 million growing about 25% this year, per the company's 8-K. Today's move puts Humana's market cap near $53 billion, per finnhub's $47.91 billion figure from before the open.

Why it matters

Medicare Advantage is the private version of Medicare for adults 65 and older, per Reuters — and star ratings are its profit engine. The ratings published Thursday don't change this year's results; they feed payments in 2028, per TradingView. That makes yesterday's release a forward-earnings event: Humana just bought itself a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual tailwind starting 2028, at the same moment it is pruning 600,000 low-margin members. Margin on a smaller, better-paid base is exactly the 3%-pretax-margin-by-2028 story management sold in July.

It also redraws the competitive map. J.P. Morgan estimates UnitedHealth's share of 4-star-or-better plans will fall to about 67% from 81%, and CVS Health's to roughly 70% from 84%, per Reuters — even as Humana's rockets to 95%. TD Cowen estimates CVS's 4.5-star-plus share fell to 35% in 2027 from 63%, per IBD. In a business where the government bonus is the margin, Humana just lapped two larger rivals.

Bull vs. bear

The bull case: Baird's math is the argument — greater than $35 in adjusted EPS in 2028, driven by conservative benefits and bids, improved star performance, and an expected deceleration in national Medicare Advantage cost trends, with "substantial upside" as investors value the stock on recovering earnings, per The Fly. Barclays' $1.5 billion EBIT / $9.00 EPS estimate for the single H5216 contract shows how much operating leverage hides in one ratings line. The stock's six-month run of 121% says the market is starting to believe it.

The bear case: Much is now priced in — the stock is up 121% in six months and 70% year-to-date, per finnhub, and Baird's $596 target implies the 2028 recovery is already substantially valued. The underlying cost pressure hasn't vanished: Humana's insurance benefit ratio was 91.2% in Q2 versus 89.9% a year earlier, and management says pharmacy cost trends remain "very elevated," driven by drug prices and new launches including GLP-1s, per the company's earnings commentary. The ratings feed 2028 payments, not today's results — and CMS can move the goalposts again, as 2024 proved.

What to watch next

  • Q3 2026 earnings (late October) — the benefit ratio and medical-cost commentary will show whether cost trends are truly stabilizing ahead of the bonus windfall.
  • The 600,000 exits — whether Humana recaptures the targeted 40% through other plans, and what the shrunken base does to 2027 revenue.
  • 2028 payment flows — when the bonus dollars actually land, and whether they match the $1.5B-EBIT arithmetic.
  • CMS methodology — any changes to how stars are calculated could replay 2024 in reverse.
  • Rival responses — whether UnitedHealth and CVS can rebuild their star mix before the next ratings cycle.

Takeaway: Humana didn't just win a ratings upgrade — it bought back the bonus payments that define its 2028 earnings power, and the market is now pricing a $35-plus EPS future.
